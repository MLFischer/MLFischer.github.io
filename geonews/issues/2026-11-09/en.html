Must-read (75+)

1. Glacial abrupt transitions in ocean circulation were much slower than ice-core records imply · Science, 2026 · Score 87 Dansgaard-Oeschger events are thought to reflect rapid shifts of the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation (AMOC); Greenland ice cores suggest warming transitions within a few decades or even just over ten years. Findings per abstract: precisely dated speleothem δ¹⁸O records from the Asian monsoon region show that the same warmings unfolded over more than a century. Isotope modelling suggests that Greenland records primarily capture a rapid atmospheric reorganisation triggered by initial sea-ice retreat in the northeastern North Atlantic, while the parallel AMOC recovery and hemispheric thermal adjustment proceeded much more slowly. Assessment: directly relevant for any analysis of transition durations, tipping points and early-warning signals in proxy series — "abrupt" depends on the archive and on which system component it records. Topic 34 · Method 22 · Venue 15 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 5 Nov 2026) · DOI: 10.1126/science.aed4270

2. Calibration guidelines and a runoff-isotope module for lake proxy system modeling (PRYSM v2.0) · Geoscientific Model Development, 2026 · Score 81 Proxy system models (PSMs) translate climate variables into proxy units and thus allow a mechanistic interpretation of lake-sediment data. PRYSM v2.0 adds a simple runoff-isotope module that can be adapted to any lake and, per abstract, reproduces the seasonal timing and magnitude at two mid-latitude lakes with contrasting morphometry, hydrology and mixing regimes. In model experiments the PSM is calibrated to a single observed profile and must predict lake-water temperature and δ²H — a typical data situation at barely monitored lakes; observations from the warm, ice-free season prove most informative. Assessment: closely related to lake water-balance and isotope models for closed basins; the calibration guidelines are transferable to unmonitored palaeolakes. Topic 38 · Method 20 · Venue 9 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 6 (created 28 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.5194/gmd-19-10243-2026

3. A Konso Silver Tuff correlative found offshore the East African Rift · Quaternary Science Reviews, 2026 · Score 79 · ⚠︎ title-based At the time of writing neither Crossref nor OpenAlex listed an abstract, and the publisher page could not be accessed. According to the title, a correlative of the Konso Silver Tuff (a tephra known from southern Ethiopia) was found outside the East African Rift, apparently in a marine archive. Assessment (with caveats): such a find could tie terrestrial rift sequences chronologically to marine records — potentially an important anchor for drill cores from southern Ethiopian rift basins. Please take age, archive and robustness of the correlation from the original paper. Relevant (50–74)

4. Forest or tundra? How different vegetation reconstructions of Last Glacial landscapes in Europe may shape our perception of early human dispersal processes · Journal of Quaternary Science, 2026 · Score 74 A comparison of four techniques — two pollen-based reconstructions and two vegetation models — for Europe between 70 and 20 ka. Findings per abstract: absolute forest cover differs by up to 40% between techniques, enough to make the difference between boreal forest and tundra; the main reason is loosely defined forest-cover metrics. All techniques, however, reproduce ecosystem dynamics over time well on continental to regional scales; the authors recommend relative forest-cover measures. Methodologically transferable to any human–environment analysis built on reconstructed landscapes. Topic 32 · Method 18 · Venue 8 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 5 Nov 2026) · DOI: 10.1002/jqs.70134

5. The Taung child and the biogeographic and palaeoclimatic drivers of southern African hominin diversity · npj Palaeoecosystems, 2026 · Score 74 The authors reassess the site's chronological age, the fossil's dental and brain development and variation of the dentino-enamel junction, and conclude that Australopithecus africanus is a poorly defined taxon. Based on new palaeoclimatic and palaeoenvironmental evidence and South Africa's position at the Zambezian–southern African biogeographic boundary, they propose that complex population dynamics (drift, hybridisation) underlie hominin morphological diversity in the Plio-Pleistocene (a hypothesis, per abstract). Connected to questions of how climatic corridors and barriers structured hominin populations. Topic 34 · Method 13 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 5 Nov 2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s44513-026-00008-2

6. The evolution of Lake Kinneret during the past 30 kyr from porewaters in the lacustrine sediments · Quaternary Science Reviews, 2026 · Score 73 · ⚠︎ title-based No abstract available. According to the title, the evolution of Lake Kinneret over 30 kyr is reconstructed from porewaters in the lake sediments — an approach that can address salinity and water balance of past lake stands directly. Thematically linked to the KIND drilling initiative (see Week 45). Findings with caveats. Topic 36 · Method 8 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 5 Nov 2026) · DOI: 10.1016/j.quascirev.2026.110357

7. Latitudinal Heterogeneity in South Asian Monsoon Changes Since the Last Glacial Maximum Revealed by Ganga–Brahmaputra Vegetation Record · Paleoceanography and Paleoclimatology, 2026 · Score 72 A new marine pollen record from the Bay of Bengal, compared with leaf-wax δ¹³C/δD and foraminiferal δ¹⁸O from the same core. Findings per abstract: an open, drier and colder landscape in MIS 2, tropical forests in MIS 1; Holocene large-scale drying with more pronounced winter dryness after 8 ka. Comparison with records farther south reveals a persistent north–south hydroclimate gradient — according to the authors pointing to additional modulation by orography and moisture recycling beyond ITCZ control. Topic 30 · Method 15 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 3 Nov 2026) · DOI: 10.1029/2026pa005425

8. Late Quaternary drainage reorganization of the Sutlej River system at the Himalaya mountain-foreland transition · Communications Earth & Environment, 2026 · Score 72 Geomorphic mapping, subsurface stratigraphy and OSL dating where the Sutlej exits the Himalayan front. Findings per abstract: a previously misidentified uplifted surface, abandoned palaeovalleys and terraces; drainage reorganisation via bifurcation and avulsion occurred between > 60 and 15 ka, tectonically controlled — long before the mid-Holocene decline of the Harappan Civilisation. An example of how fluvial archives help separate tectonic from climatic control. Topic 30 · Method 15 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 7 Nov 2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04204-5

9. Fragmentation of AMOC tipping · Dialogues on Climate Change, 2026 · Score 69 Commentary (no new data): ocean models indicate that the AMOC may undergo several partial collapses before a full collapse; in a conceptual box model this fragmentation complicates the interpretation of early-warning signals. The piece discusses whether these intermediate transitions survive in more detailed models — particularly because the convection parameterisation can introduce additional, possibly spurious branches in the bifurcation diagram — and calls for convergence tests. Conceptually relevant for tipping-point analyses of palaeorecords. Topic 30 · Method 18 · Venue 5 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 4 Nov 2026) · DOI: 10.1177/29768659261502897

10. Sequential high-density radiocarbon dating to detect invisible event layers in marine sediments · Radiocarbon, 2026 · Score 67 Continuous ¹⁴C dating of total organic carbon at 0.5–1 cm intervals: age reversals mark, per abstract, event layers from submarine slope failures rather than flood input; after a foraminiferal age model, the layers correlate with historical natural disasters since the 15th century. Methodologically interesting for detecting visually inconspicuous event layers, including in palaeoflood archives. Topic 25 · Method 18 · Venue 8 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 9 Nov 2026) · DOI: 10.1017/rdc.2026.10255

What this means for coverage. Where neither OpenAlex nor Crossref provides an abstract, the entry is marked ⚠︎ title-based; no findings beyond the title were invented, and methodological fit receives minimum points. Dates are Crossref created dates (registration), which may differ from the online publication date by a few days. Topic queries return a lot of off-topic noise; pre-selection used keywords followed by manual review, so individual relevant papers outside the core journals may be missing. Listed papers were checked for co-authorship by the publisher of this digest; there is none this week.
