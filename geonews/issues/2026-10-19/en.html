What is GeoNews? An automatically compiled weekly digest. A research workflow scans publications from the past one to two weeks as well as funding, policy and community sources, scores the hits against a fixed set of criteria and assembles this issue from them.

The thematic profile follows the research interests of Markus L. Fischer (Palaeoclimate Dynamics group, University of Potsdam): Quaternary palaeoclimate and palaeoenvironmental reconstruction, the East African Rift, the African Humid Period and human–environment coupling, lake-level and water-balance modelling, palaeofloods and fluvial geomorphology, ICDP drill cores, and linear and nonlinear time-series analysis. The score measures proximity to this newsletter's profile and prioritises reading — it is not a quality judgement on the individual paper.

In brief

About three dozen scored papers, with four must-reads this week, all based on abstracts. There is no direct primary hit from the East African Rift this time; the emphasis is on time-series methods applied to lake and cave archives and on drill cores from Alpine lakes.

At the top is a Quaternary Science Reviews study that uses an EMD variant and the Hilbert transform on five high-resolution lake records from China to quantify decadal dry–wet shifts over the last millennium; according to the abstract, their frequency increases with the contrast of the centennial background state. The other : an ICDP core from the Lago Maggiore system with an abrupt lake-level drop at ~9 ka, the non-stationary coupling of the East Asian summer monsoon to the Pacific and North Atlantic from three laminated stalagmites, and Etheria elliptica shells from the middle Atbara River (eastern Sudan, draining the Ethiopian Highlands), which according to the abstract point to precipitation seasonality persisting for at least ~200 kyr.

In the block: an abrupt regime shift to a hotter, drier climate in the Western Eurasian Steppe (Nature Geoscience; early-warning framing), a pollen-based LGM-to-present reconstruction from an annually laminated maar lake, proxy-withholding experiments on the weight of individual archive types in palaeo data assimilation, demographic responses to the Kikai-Akahoya eruption, and a new 1.5-Myr compilation of glacial and interglacial intensities across the Mid-Pleistocene Transition.

On funding, with the ERC Starting Grant deadline (14 Oct) passed, the ERC Consolidator Grant (12 Jan 2027) is the next major date; the Horizon Europe palaeoclimate call opens on 17 Nov 2026. On policy: the WissZeitVG reform had its first reading in the Bundestag on 8 Oct 2026, and the federal government rejects the Bundesrat's central demand. The European Council continued negotiations on the 2028–2034 MFF on 15–16 Oct — the aim remains an agreement by year-end.

Must-read (75 and above)

1. Tracking decadal dry–wet shifts over the past millennium in China: Insights from five high-resolution lake records · Quaternary Science Reviews, 2026 · Score 87 Five well-dated, high-resolution lacustrine precipitation records (Gonghai, Nvshan, Jiangcuo, Kusai, Sihailongwan) are decomposed using regenerated phase-shifted sinusoid-assisted empirical mode decomposition (RPSEMD) combined with the Hilbert transform to extract decadal hydroclimate variability and identify dry–wet shifts quantitatively. Findings according to the abstract: regional coherence alongside local divergence — more frequent shifts during the Medieval Climate Anomaly at Gonghai, but concentrated in the Little Ice Age at Nvshan, Kusai and Sihailongwan; little difference at Jiangcuo. The frequency of decadal shifts correlates positively with the contrast of the centennial background state; the authors conclude that longer-term states may influence the frequency and magnitude of higher-frequency variability (interpretation; mechanism open, with roles for the monsoon, westerlies and local hydrology). Methodologically transferable to high-resolution rift-lake records: the same question of state-dependent variability that tipping-point and regime analyses ask. Topic 38 · Method 22 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 14 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1016/j.quascirev.2026.110338

2. New constraints on the postglacial evolution of Lago Maggiore, Northern Italy · Journal of Quaternary Science, 2026 · Score 78 A 58-m ICDP core from the lower Ossola Valley (an overdeepened valley at the southern margin of the Alps, ~500 m of infill), studied with sedimentology, geotechnics, luminescence and radiocarbon dating, and placed in a reflection-seismic context. Findings according to the abstract: a coarse basal interval overlain by ~35 m of silts and sands representing an ice-retreat and glaciolacustrine sequence; a distinct transition to sandier, more heterogeneous lake deposits is attributed to a dam breach with lake-level drop and sudden delta progradation at ca. 9 ka. A clustering of mass movements in other Alpine lakes at 9.5–10 ka might point to a major earthquake as a common source (framed by the authors as a possibility); at ca. 7 ka the site fell dry. The causes of the lake-level drops remain open according to the abstract. Relevant for ICDP lake-core chronologies and Central European lake-sediment archives. Topic 36 · Method 16 · Venue 8 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 12 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1002/jqs.70122

3. Non-stationary evolution of centennial to decadal East Asian summer monsoon variability modulated by trans-oceanic interactions over the last millennium · Quaternary Science Reviews, 2026 · Score 77 δ¹⁸O records from three annually laminated, replicated stalagmites from eastern China covering the last millennium. Findings according to the abstract: segmented correlation analysis reveals four stages in the spatial pattern of monsoon rainfall, synchronised with Northern Hemisphere temperature (meridional displacement of the frontal rainfall belt). The first principal component is non-stationary: during warm periods a 20–40-year periodicity linked to the PDO dominates; in the Little Ice Age this coupling weakens and a ~50-year periodicity linked to Atlantic Multidecadal Variability emerges. CESM Last Millennium Ensemble simulations attribute this Atlantic variability mainly to volcanic forcing. Relevant as an example of time-dependent spectral and coupling analysis on proxy series — methodologically close to nonlinear time-series analysis. Topic 30 · Method 20 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 13 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1016/j.quascirev.2026.110309

4. Paleoseasonality through serial stable isotope analysis of Pleistocene to recent Etheria elliptica shells from the middle Atbara River, eastern Sudan · E&G Quaternary Science Journal, 2026 · Score 76 Serial δ¹⁸O and δ¹³C analyses of 11 fossil and three modern shells of the freshwater oyster Etheria elliptica from the middle Atbara River (eastern Sudan; the Atbara drains the Ethiopian Highlands), with the fossils OSL-dated to ~224 to ~17 ka, plus a local meteoric water line from seven river-water samples. Findings according to the abstract: fossil shells are enriched in δ¹⁸O (possibly drier, more evaporative Pleistocene conditions — though the authors also name dams as a cause of more depleted modern values); δ¹³C differences are linked in part to atmospheric CO₂, with alternative explanations (vegetation, weathering, biological factors) left open. The authors' overall conclusion: strong precipitation seasonality has likely persisted in eastern Sudan since at least ~200 ka. Regionally connected to highland runoff records and to the question of how far seasonality and annual means varied separately during north-east African humid phases. Topic 38 · Method 13 · Venue 7 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 14 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.5194/egqsj-75-173-2026

Relevant (50–74)

5. An abrupt shift towards a hotter and drier climate regime in the Western Eurasian Steppe · Nature Geoscience, 2026 · Score 74 Reanalysis data plus large-ensemble and multi-model simulations identify an abrupt regime shift in the Western Eurasian Steppe, with unprecedented summer drying beyond the range of natural variability of the past century and no clear recovery over the observational period (1901–2024). According to the abstract, external forcing provides the background drying while internal variability amplified the trend since the early 1990s; declining soil moisture intensifies heat and deficits through land–atmosphere feedback. The authors present the region as an early warning signal for other mid-latitude drylands. Not a palaeo paper — but the same question as in palaeo tipping-point research: when does a trend become a persistent change of state? Topic 28 · Method 15 · Venue 15 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 12 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s41561-026-02125-8

6. High-Resolution Temperature and Precipitation Variability in Northeast Asia Since the Last Glacial Maximum Recorded in an Annually Laminated Maar Lake · Journal of Geophysical Research: Atmospheres, 2026 · Score 74 Pollen-based reconstructions of mean annual temperature and precipitation from Xiaolongwan maar lake from the LGM to the present. Findings according to the abstract: increases of ~9.0 ± 2.2 °C and ~470 ± 121 mm from the LGM to the mid-Holocene; between ~19.0 and 17.0 kyr b2k temperature drops markedly while precipitation stays within uncertainty — a decoupling the authors attribute to different forcings (high latitudes vs. monsoon/western Pacific SST). Across the Bølling-Allerød, Younger Dryas and early Holocene both shift rapidly and largely simultaneously. Relevant for proxy approaches that implicitly assume temperature and precipitation co-vary. Topic 34 · Method 13 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 12 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1029/2026jd047113

7. Differential Impacts of Proxy Archives on Paleoclimate Reconstruction Performance · Journal of Geophysical Research: Atmospheres, 2026 · Score 73 Proxy-withholding experiments in palaeo data assimilation quantify the marginal contribution of eight archive types (corals, tree rings, speleothems, documentary records, lake sediments, marine sediments, glaciers, ground ice) to ENSO, the Indian Ocean Dipole, global mean temperature and global mean precipitation. Findings according to the abstract: assimilation consistently improves skill, with the multi-model ensemble as the most stable prior; corals are indispensable for all indices, while documentary records and speleothems add key information in monsoon-sensitive, data-sparse regions; some archives have outsized leverage relative to their coverage. Relevant to the question of how much additional lake-sediment records from data-sparse regions such as East Africa would add to reconstructions. Topic 30 · Method 16 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 12 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1029/2025jd045958

8. Divergent demographic responses to the Kikai-Akahoya volcanic catastrophe in mid-Holocene Japan · Science Advances, 2026 · Score 71 Statistical models of the demographic and environmental consequences of the K-Ah eruption (~7300 years ago) for Jōmon communities. Findings according to the abstract: population collapse in regions immediately adjacent to the volcano, but increases in some proximal populations; some distal communities were affected by previously overlooked environmental consequences. The authors read this as a contingent rather than deterministic response to a shock. Methodologically transferable to human–environment questions at wet–dry transitions (e.g. the end of the African Humid Period). Topic 25 · Method 15 · Venue 15 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 14 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aeh7197

9. Glacial and interglacial intensities across the Mid-Pleistocene Transition from a new 1.5 Myr long compilation of marine and terrestrial records · EGUsphere (preprint, in discussion), 2026 · Score 70 A compilation of 29 palaeoclimate records over 1.5 Myr, half marine and half terrestrial, with intensity measures for each glacial and interglacial. Findings according to the abstract: glacial amplification from MIS 22 (~930 ka) in both marine and terrestrial archives; no consistent trend in interglacial strength in terrestrial records; MIS 13a weak in marine but strong in several terrestrial records; since the Mid-Brunhes Shift a linear relationship between interglacial strength and the preceding glacial in benthic δ¹⁸O, but not in other archive types. Comes with an interactive dashboard. Relevant for long terrestrial drill-core records and their synchronisation. Not yet peer-reviewed. Topic 34 · Method 16 · Venue 4 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 13 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.5194/egusphere-2026-6154

10. Late Quaternary evolution of river Nile at Wadi Kubbaniya, Egypt: Insights from mineralogy and geochemistry proxies · Journal of African Earth Sciences, 2026 · Score 70 · ⚠︎ title-based Late Quaternary evolution of the Nile at Wadi Kubbaniya (Upper Egypt) from mineralogical and geochemical proxies. Part C — Science policy (DE/EU)

WissZeitVG — first reading in the Bundestag, government rejects Bundesrat demand. The government bill on "modernising fixed-term employment law in science" (Bundestag printed paper 21/8281 of 30 Sep 2026) had its first reading on 8 Oct 2026 and was referred, together with a Die Linke motion to abolish the special fixed-term regime (21/8384), to the research committee. Core elements: minimum terms for first contracts (three years before, two years after the doctorate), family-related extensions also for third-party-funded contracts, a new two-year care extension, at least a quarter of regular working time for qualification contracts, maximum duration for student assistants of eight instead of six years, evaluation in 2032. The government rejects the Bundesrat's demand that contracts below the minimum term be treated as running for that minimum, pointing to general labour law instead. The research committee's agenda of 14 Oct 2026 included a decision on holding a public hearing. Assessment: for third-party-funded postdoc positions with short project durations, the minimum term remains the most consequential lever in practice. Methods & sources

Paper screening follows the fixed criteria (topical fit, methodological fit, impact/venue, connectivity, recency) and excludes DOIs already listed in a previous issue. Funding opportunities, science policy and dates are based on the sources linked in Parts B to D.

How this issue was produced. Direct network access from the computing environment was blocked. OpenAlex (ISSN queries of the core journals with from_publication_date:2026-10-05, topic and title searches; abstracts reconstructed from the abstract_inverted_index) and the Crossref REST API (ISSN queries including Quaternary International, The Holocene, J. African Earth Sci., Geomorphology, J. Human Evolution, GRL, G-cubed, Paleoceanography and Paleoclimatology; topic queries; created dates of all listed papers) were queried via the integrated browser interface (fetch()); one abstract was taken from the publisher page (Nature Portfolio). ScienceDirect landing pages were not reachable.

What this means for coverage. Where neither OpenAlex nor Crossref holds an abstract, the entry is marked ⚠︎ title-based; no results beyond the title were invented. Dates given are Crossref created dates (registration), which may differ from the online publication date by a few days. Topic queries return a lot of off-topic noise; pre-selection was by keywords and then manual, so individual relevant papers outside the core journals may be missing.
