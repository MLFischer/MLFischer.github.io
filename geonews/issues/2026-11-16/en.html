What is GeoNews? An automatically generated weekly digest. A research workflow scans publications from the past one to two weeks as well as funding, policy and community sources, scores the hits against a fixed set of criteria and assembles this issue from them.

The thematic profile is aligned with the research interests of Markus L. Categories: 75+ must-read · 50–74 relevant · 30–49 radar. The score measures proximity to this newsletter's profile and prioritises reading — it is not a quality judgement on the individual paper.

In brief

About 40 scored papers, this week with four must-reads, all with an accessible abstract. Focal points: threshold-like climate responses to orbital forcing, hominins at Pleistocene palaeolakes, dating Pleistocene groundwater and decadal lake-level variability.

At the top: quantitative brGDGT reconstructions of temperature and monsoon precipitation from the Chinese Loess Plateau over ~250 kyr reveal repeated extreme cold–dry events at glacial inceptions, which model simulations reproduce as a threshold-like response to declining boreal summer insolation (Commun. Earth Environ.). Also a ~125 ka Middle Palaeolithic site on the Mundafan palaeolake (Saudi Arabia) with evidence of poisoned weapons and megafauna use (Science), the extension of ⁸¹Kr dating down to 10 ka, which independently tests ¹⁴C ages of Pleistocene groundwater (Sci. Adv.), and a 2500-year diatom salinity record from an Australian maar lake showing shifts between wet and dry states on multidecadal to multicentennial timescales (EGUsphere, preprint).

In the block, among others: shifts in Greenland dust provenance during Dansgaard–Oeschger cycles (larger Saharan share in stadials, PNAS), pastoralism in Africa millennia before farming (QSR, ⚠︎ title-based), a Middle Pleistocene switch from rainforest to desert in eastern Australia, and "generational amnesia" in historical climate archives (Nat. Must-read (75+)

1. Extreme cold–dry events in East Asia during glacials reflect insolation thresholds · Communications Earth & Environment, 2026 · Score 83 Whether climate extremes reflect intrinsic threshold behaviour of the climate system is unresolved — partly because most terrestrial archives integrate several signals. The authors use brGDGT proxies to reconstruct land temperature and monsoon precipitation on the Chinese Loess Plateau over ~250,000 years at ~900-year resolution. Finding according to the abstract: repeated extreme cold–dry events at glacial inceptions and pronounced droughts at terminations, which are not clearly expressed in speleothem δ¹⁸O records. Transient simulations reproduce the timing of the cold events, which the authors take as support for a threshold-like response to declining boreal summer insolation. Assessment: methodologically directly relevant to tipping-point and regime-shift analyses of palaeo time series; whether similar thresholds occur in African monsoon archives is an obvious question but not addressed in the paper. Topic 36 · Method 20 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 10 Nov 2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04060-3

2. Poisoned hunting weapons, megafauna, and plant use at a 125,000-year-old Arabian paleolakeshore · Science, 2026 · Score 78 The site of ‘Uruq Aljelibah lies on the shore of the Mundafan palaeolake (Saudi Arabia) and is dated to ~125 ka (MIS 5e). According to the abstract, Levallois assemblages, refits and megafaunal associations indicate on-site tool production and carcass processing; residues, use-wear and biomarkers document animal and plant processing (including palms and tubers). Several points carry impact damage and poison residues (Acokanthera schimperi) and are interpreted as hafted weapon tips. The fauna (hippopotamus, buffalo, gazelle, ostrich, camel) points to freshwater and grassland ecosystems. Assessment: a data point for hominin dispersal during humid phases on the Arabian Peninsula — closely related to human–environment coupling during African humid periods. Topic 34 · Method 13 · Venue 15 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 12 Nov 2026) · DOI: 10.1126/science.aef9342

3. High-precision ⁸¹Kr dating overcomes radiocarbon limits in Late Pleistocene groundwater · Science Advances, 2026 · Score 77 Groundwater preserves continental hydroclimate signals, but its use as a Late Pleistocene archive is often limited by uncertainties in ¹⁴C correction. Using high-precision atom trap trace analysis, the authors lower the ⁸¹Kr dating limit to ~10 ka. Finding according to the abstract (North China Plain): between 10 and 30 ka, ⁸¹Kr independently validates ¹⁴C ages, and beyond that it provides reliable ages for older water; the dual-tracer approach reduces model uncertainty and flags mixed samples. After screening, groundwater δ¹⁸O closely tracks polar ice-core records. Assessment: an independent age framework for palaeohydrology in basins that lack conventional archives. Topic 28 · Method 18 · Venue 15 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 13 Nov 2026) · DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aef6864

4. Common Era hydroclimate in south-eastern Australia: a decadal-scale diatom-inferred salinity record from Lake Bullen Merri · EGUsphere (preprint, not peer-reviewed), 2026 · Score 76 Sub-decadal to decadal reconstruction of lake-water salinity (electrical conductivity, diatom-inferred) over ~2500 years from a ~58 m deep maar lake; conductivity is inversely related to lake volume. Chronology from nine AMS ¹⁴C dates on pollen concentrates, ²¹⁰Pb, plutonium and the first appearance of Pinus pollen. Finding according to the abstract: a prolonged high stand ~1380–1750 CE (Little Ice Age), preceded by three prominent centennial-scale low stands; neighbouring maar lakes show coherent patterns. The wet/dry contrast is barely visible in existing tree-ring and ice-core reconstructions, most of which begin during the wet phase. Assessment: an example of how closed lakes capture the full range of hydroclimatic states that shorter series miss — transferable to the question of drought recurrence in rift lakes. Topic 36 · Method 18 · Venue 4 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 13 Nov 2026) · DOI: 10.5194/egusphere-2026-6649

Millennial-scale variability in Greenland dust provenance during Dansgaard–Oeschger events of the last glacial period · PNAS, 2026 · Score 74 Pb and Sr isotopes, mineralogy and geochemistry from the NEEM ice core across the entire last glacial (~800-year resolution). 7. Oxygen isotopes reveal a significant Middle Pleistocene transition from temperate rainforest to arid desert-like conditions in eastern Australia · Quaternary Science Reviews, 2026 · Score 73 δ¹⁸O of fossil kangaroo enamel from the Mount Etna Caves (Queensland) combined with refined in-situ U-Th dating. Finding according to the abstract: the arid fauna is constrained to 199–205 ka; older deposits (pre-280 ka) show low δ¹⁸O values, younger ones markedly enriched values. Random-forest classifiers trained on modern data assign the older assemblages to humid temperate and the younger ones to hot desert climates. The authors interpret this as the crossing of hydroclimatic thresholds during the Middle Pleistocene reorganisation around ~270 ka (interpretation). Topic 30 · Method 16 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 13 Nov 2026) · DOI: 10.1016/j.quascirev.2026.110367

8. Archival and palaeoenvironmental documentation of extreme climate events reveals generational amnesia in collective memory · Nature Communications, 2026 · Score 70 Historical climate archives from northern China (1470–1900) are compared with tree-ring hydroclimate reconstructions. Finding according to the abstract: the severity of extremes is systematically judged relative to recent experience; correlations increase when the natural series is re-standardised in 20–40-year sliding windows. An agent-based model reproduces the effect. Methodologically relevant to any calibration of documentary flood and drought series. Topic 25 · Method 18 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 9 Nov 2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-78922-x

9. Hydro-ecological responses to AMOC in North China during Heinrich 4 and 5 stadials · Quaternary Science Reviews, 2026 · Score 69 · ⚠︎ Title-based No abstract available. According to the title, the paper examines hydro-ecological responses in North China to AMOC weakening during Heinrich stadials 4 and 5 — teleconnections of abrupt ocean-circulation changes to continental hydrology. Findings with reservations. Topic 34 · Method 8 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 9 Nov 2026) · DOI: 10.1016/j.quascirev.2026.110370

10. High sedimentation rates drove the transformation from lake to peatland in Jinchuan (Northeastern China) during the Holocene Climate Optimum · Quaternary Science Reviews, 2026 · Score 67 · ⚠︎ Title-based No abstract available. Part C — Science policy (DE/EU)

WissZeitVG — Bundestag passes the amendment on 12 Nov 2026. Adopted in the version of the research committee with the votes of CDU/CSU, SPD and AfD against Die Linke, with the Greens abstaining (printed papers 21/8281, 21/9179). Key points according to the Bundestag and the government bill: minimum durations for first contracts of three years before and two years after the PhD; the qualification goal must be stated explicitly in the contract (committee amendment); a uniform six-year maximum for fixed-term postdoc contracts (medicine loses its previous nine years); third-party-funded contracts should match the approved project period and can be extended for maternity protection and parental leave; an additional two years per phase for caring for relatives; evaluation only six years after entry into force. Entry into force on the first day of the sixth calendar month after promulgation; the Bundesrat date could not be verified at the time of writing. The "Tarifsperre" (bar on collective agreements deviating from the law) remains — ver.di calls the reform a "missed opportunity" and regards minimum durations and extension rights as only a partial success. Assessment: for third-party-funded positions, the link to project duration and the family provisions are the most immediately relevant; the six-year limit after the PhD remains the key planning parameter for postdoc careers. Sources: Bundestag — Reform of fixed-term contract law (week 46) · BMFTR news item (12 Nov 2026) · Government bill, Drs. 21/8281 · ver.di (13 Nov 2026).

Methods & sources

Paper screening follows the fixed set of criteria (topical fit, methodological fit, impact/venue, connectivity, recency) and excludes DOIs already listed in a previous issue. Funding opportunities, science policy and dates are based on the sources linked in Parts B to D.

How this issue was produced. Direct network access from the computing environment was blocked. The Crossref REST API was queried via the integrated browser interface (fetch()): ISSN queries for around 30 journals (including QSR, Climate of the Past, Quat. Sci. Adv., GPC, EPSL, Boreas, JQS, Quat. Res., P³, Nature, Science, PNAS, Nature Commun., Nature Geosci., Sci. Adv., Commun. Earth Environ., GRL, G-cubed, Paleoceanogr. Paleoclimatol., Quat. Int., The Holocene, Geology, J. African Earth Sci., J. Human Evol., Radiocarbon), the Copernicus prefix 10.5194 including EGUsphere, and 15 topic queries, each with the filter from-created-date:2026-11-02. OpenAlex did not yet provide abstracts for the very recently registered Elsevier titles.

What this means for coverage. Where no abstract was available, the entry is marked ⚠︎ title-based; no findings beyond the title were invented, and methodological fit is scored with minimum points. For very high-volume journals (including Nature Commun., PNAS, Sci. Adv.) and the Copernicus prefix, the queries hit result caps, so individual relevant papers may be missing. The dates given are Crossref created dates (registration), which may differ from the online publication date by a few days. Pre-selection from the topic queries was done by keyword and then manually. Listed papers were checked for co-authorship by the editor; there is none this week.
