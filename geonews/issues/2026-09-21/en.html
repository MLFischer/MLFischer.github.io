In brief

Twenty-one scored papers, and one must-read this week. At the top is a peer-reviewed, open-access Communications Earth & Environment study of the Pacific Walker Circulation over the past 800,000 years: an ENSO-Modoki index is reconstructed from sea-surface temperature gradients across the equatorial Pacific, with a documented shift of the mean state after about 250 ka — driven by orbital and sea-level forcing. Exactly the combination of a long Quaternary proxy series and a non-linear regime shift that sits at the centre of this profile.

Regionally notable is an Early Pleistocene isotope study from the Upper Awash Valley (Ethiopia) in the East African Rift. Must-read (from 75)

1. Coupled orbital and sea-level forcing on the Pacific Walker Circulation during the past 800 kyrs · Communications Earth & Environment, 2026 · Score 88 Reconstruction of an ENSO-Modoki index over the past 800,000 years from sea-surface temperature gradients across the equatorial Pacific. Per the abstract: glacial periods favoured El Niño Modoki-like conditions, while the periodic submersion of the Sunda Shelf after about 250 ka shifted the mean state during interglacials toward La Niña Modoki-like conditions. The authors read this as the first evidence that the ENSO-Modoki configuration has been a persistent feature of Pacific climate, and emphasise the long-term sensitivity of the Walker Circulation to orbital and sea-level forcing. Squarely in the profile centre: a Quaternary palaeoclimate reconstruction over a long proxy series with a documented shift of the mean state (regime shift) — the same question of thresholds and non-linear system responses that is central to tipping-point and time-series analysis. Open access (Nature portfolio). Theme 37 · Method 21 · Venue 12 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 17.09.2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04062-1

Relevant (50–74)

2. Simultaneous Megapluvials in Southwestern North and South America During the Last Millennium · Paleoceanography and Paleoclimatology, 2026 · Score 73 Analysis of decadal wet and dry extremes (megapluvials, megadroughts) in the North American Southwest and South American Southwest over the past millennium, using a palaeoclimate data-assimilation product. Per the abstract: 18 megapluvials per region (12 overlapping) and 13 vs. 15 megadroughts (9 overlapping), of similar duration and severity. Megapluvials are driven primarily by a reduction in drying La Niña-like states, not solely by an increase in wetting El Niño-like states; large volcanic eruptions shift the frequencies toward wetting via the ENSO teleconnection, most clearly in the South American Southwest. Connective as a palaeohydrological extreme-value and teleconnection study: identifying and attributing prolonged hydroclimatic extremes touches the same methodological question as Central European palaeoflood and water-balance archives. Theme 26 · Method 18 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 16.09.2026) · DOI: 10.1029/2025pa005399

3. Lebanese faunal legacy collections uncover 400,000 years of woodland resilience and distinct hominin ecological strategies · Communications Earth & Environment, 2026 · Score 72 Reassessment of three legacy faunal collections from Lebanon (Naame, Nahr Ibrahim, Ras el-Kelb) with, for the first time, direct dates, zooarchaeological methods, dental-wear analysis and landscape modelling. Per the abstract: one of the longest palaeoecological records in the region (roughly 400,000 to 100,000 years); productive Mediterranean woodland ecosystems persisted along the central Levantine coast throughout much of the Middle and Late Pleistocene, supporting diverse ungulate communities and enabling hominins to practise seasonal, habitat-specific hunting along coastal ecotones. The authors stress a marked ecological differentiation between the central and southern Levant. Connective as a Quaternary human–environment study in the Levantine dispersal corridor (Tier 2); it complements last week's central-Levantine enamel-isotope paper (different DOI, related topic). Theme 28 · Method 15 · Venue 12 · Connectivity 7 · Recency 10 (created 14.09.2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-03926-w

4. Comparing the Simulated Surface Mass Balance of the Laurentide Ice Sheet During the Last Deglaciation With a Geophysical Reconstruction · Paleoceanography and Paleoclimatology, 2026 · Score 69 Calculation of the surface mass balance (SMB) of the Laurentide Ice Sheet during the last deglaciation from the isotope-enabled transient climate model iTraCE, compared with the ICE-6G reconstruction (LGM to 12 ka). Per the abstract: the iTraCE SMB overestimates mass loss between 20 and 18 ka (about a 60 % excess in cumulative loss) and aligns more closely with ICE-6G after 18 ka; including meltwater forcing produces abrupt SMB shifts and improves agreement during Heinrich Stadial 1 and the Younger Dryas — evidence for the role of ocean circulation and sea-ice feedbacks in modulating melt rates. Connective as a deglacial ice-sheet/climate-model study involving abrupt events (Tier 2). Theme 25 · Method 16 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 7 · Recency 10 (created 21.09.2026) · DOI: 10.1029/2025pa005366

5. Dental enamel hypoplasia and intra-tooth isotope records as indicators of physiological stress and environmental variability in Early Pleistocene hippopotamids from Melka Kunture (Upper Awash, Ethiopia) · Journal of Quaternary Science, 2026 · Score 68 Combination of macroscopic linear enamel hypoplasia (LEH) with intra-tooth δ¹³C/δ¹⁸O isotope profiles (100 sequential samples) on two Early Pleistocene hippopotamid teeth from Melka Kunture in the Upper Awash Valley (Ethiopia), dated to about 1.4 and 1.0 Ma. Per the abstract: both teeth show LEH but with different distributions and contrasting intra-tooth isotopic patterns; in the older tooth (~1.4 Ma) δ¹³C and δ¹⁸O correlate strongly and positively, with higher values within the LEH interval than in the unaffected apical enamel — consistent with discrete stress episodes under changing hydrological conditions. Regionally in the profile centre (East African Rift, Upper Awash) and methodologically connective: seasonally resolved isotopy to reconstruct short-term environmental variability, as is also central to continental rift archives. Theme 30 · Method 16 · Venue 8 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 6 (created 07.09.2026) · DOI: 10.1002/jqs.70113

6. Unveiling Arctic Organic Carbon Cycling Through Amino Acid Radiocarbon Activity: Implications for Paleoclimatic Reconstruction · Paleoceanography and Paleoclimatology, 2026 · Score 67 Development of a chronological method: the radiocarbon ages of total hydrolysable amino acids (THAA) serve as a dating tool for sedimentary organic carbon in the Arctic Ocean (core R14, Chukchi Plateau), with a continuous age framework across the last deglaciation. Per the abstract: the THAA ¹⁴C chronology agrees with published Marine Isotope Stage records; different organic components show markedly different "pre-depositional" ages — during marine transgression, substantially older total-OC and humic-acid ages (intensified coastal erosion), younger lipid ages around the Younger Dryas and the Bering Strait opening, and minimum ages of the acid-base-insoluble fraction in the mid-Holocene. Connective as a methodological chronology paper (Tier 2), akin to Bayesian age-depth questions. Part C — Science policy (DE/EU)

WissZeitVG — amendment in parliamentary deliberation. Following the cabinet decision of 29.07.2026, the draft law to modernise fixed-term employment law in academia has been in parliamentary deliberation since the summer break; after the first reading it sits in the Committee on Education, Research and Technology Assessment. The core of the draft is, for the first time, binding minimum contract durations: three years for first contracts of doctoral candidates, two years for postdocs; the maximum fixed-term duration in the postdoc phase is unified at six years. For student assistants a minimum duration of one year and an increase of the maximum fixed-term duration from six to eight years are envisaged. As interpretation, not fact: minimum durations improve the plannability of early contracts, while unifying the postdoc maximum narrows individual carry-over latitude; the further parliamentary path and timetable are open. Sources: BMFTR — Cabinet adopts WissZeitVG amendment · Forschung & Lehre — WissZeitVG focus.

Federal budget 2027 — cabinet decision of 06.07.2026, parliamentary deliberation in autumn. The government draft has stood since 6 July 2026. The BMFTR core budget is around €22 billion — a nominal increase of only about 0.7 percent over 2026. Real growth comes almost exclusively from the special fund for infrastructure and climate neutrality, whose R&D investments rise markedly in 2027 over 2026. The first reading in the Bundestag is due in autumn. As interpretation: the regular budget stagnates in real terms given current pay and material-cost increases, keeping the operating situation of the funding organisations under pressure. Sources: Forschung & Lehre — Only a slim increase for the BMFTR core budget · Table.Briefings — Budget 2027.

DFG cuts at the postdoc level remain in force. Because of high pay increases and rising equipment and consumable costs that the promised annual funding increments do not cover, the DFG has postponed the funding start of establishing applications for Research Training Groups and suspended the approval of postdoc positions. For postdoc career paths in the geosciences a funding path thus falls away for now, increasing pressure on individual-grant and EU funds. Source: Fakultätentag Informatik.
