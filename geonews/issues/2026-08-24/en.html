What is GeoNews? An automatically generated weekly digest. A research workflow scans the publications of the past one to two weeks along with funding, policy and community sources, scores the hits against a fixed set of criteria, and assembles this issue from them.

Selection, scoring and short texts are produced by machine and reviewed before publication. Details may contain errors; in case of doubt the linked original source prevails.

Categories: from 75 must-read · 50–74 relevant · 30–49 radar. The score measures proximity to this newsletter's profile and prioritises reading — it is not a quality judgement on the individual paper.

Relevant (50–74)

1. Increased abyssal ocean density stratification across the Middle Pleistocene Transition · Climate of the Past, 2026 · Score 69 A reconstruction of interbasinal deep-sea temperature and salinity gradients over the past 1.5 million years, based on stacked Mg/Ca and benthic δ¹⁸O datasets. Result: across the Middle Pleistocene Transition (MPT) the deep Atlantic water mass cooled while the Pacific became more saline — deep-ocean density stratification increased. The authors argue that the glacial ocean thereby became a more effective carbon trap, which lowered atmospheric pCO₂ and favoured the growth of larger ice sheets; they read this as a physical contribution to explaining the MPT. Connective as a Quaternary palaeoceanography study (Tier 2): the MPT is one of the central reorganisations of the Quaternary climate system, and the question of how ocean stratification, the carbon cycle and ice volume interact touches the same forcing decomposition that is also central to continental water-balance and monsoon dynamics across the glacial–interglacial cycle. Methodologically a clean example of stacking and interbasinal differencing of long proxy time series. Theme 25 · Method 17 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (publ. 19 Aug 2026) · DOI: 10.5194/cp-22-1559-2026

2. Sustained Late-Nineteenth-Century Monsoon Drought in Seoul and the Indian Ocean–North Atlantic Forcing of Its Circulation Weakening · EGUsphere (preprint, under review for Climate of the Past), 2026 · Score 60 An analysis of one of the world's longest daily precipitation series: the Korean royal chronicles preserve rain-gauge readings for Seoul back to 1777. After checking doubtful entries against the original manuscripts, the study documents a sustained drought — summer rainfall fell by about a quarter over 27 years (1881–1907). Climate model experiments, per the abstract, trace the cause to a weakened monsoon circulation driven mainly by unusually cool waters in the Indian Ocean and North Atlantic. Connective as a palaeohydrological / climate-dynamics study (Tier 2): multidecadal rainfall persistence and its remote coupling to oceanic boundary conditions are methodologically the same attribution question about long-lasting dry phases that is central in continental hydroclimate archives — here through a documentary instrumental series rather than a sediment archive. Preprint, hence the low venue weight; the finding stands provisionally pending completion of review. Theme 25 · Method 15 · Venue 4 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (publ. 18 Aug 2026) · DOI: 10.5194/egusphere-2026-4812

Part C — Science policy (DE/EU)

WissZeitVG — amendment adopted by the cabinet on 29 Jul 2026, Bundestag to follow in the autumn. The federal cabinet adopted the bill to modernise fixed-term employment law in science on 29 July 2026; the May draft was revised in parts after criticism from associations. At its core are, for the first time, binding minimum contract durations: three years for doctoral researchers' first contracts, two years for postdocs; the maximum fixed-term duration in the postdoc phase is standardised at six years. For student research assistants the draft provides a minimum term of one year and raises the maximum fixed-term duration from six to eight years. According to parliamentary sources, the Bundestag debate is to be expected after the summer recess, i.e. in September. As interpretation, not established fact: minimum durations improve the predictability of early contracts, while the removal of earlier carry-over flexibility further narrows individual fixed-term planning in the postdoc phase — the further parliamentary path remains to be watched. Sources: BMFTR — Cabinet adopts amendment to the WissZeitVG · Bundestag — Reform of the Wissenschaftszeitvertragsgesetz · Wiarda blog — WissZeitVG: BMFTR presents draft.

DFG cuts at the postdoc level remain in force. Owing to high pay rises and increased equipment and consumables costs that the promised annual funding increments do not cover, the DFG has postponed the funding start of new Research Training Group applications and suspended the approval of postdoc positions. Methods & sources

The paper review follows the fixed set of criteria (thematic proximity, methodological proximity, impact/venue, connectivity, recency) and excludes DOIs already listed in an earlier issue. Funding opportunities, science policy and dates rely on the sources linked in Parts B to D.

On how this issue came about — a different research path. Crossref access from the compute environment was blocked as in previous weeks (proxy 403); in addition, the browser-based query interface otherwise used as a fallback was unavailable in this automated run. The systematic ISSN query of the core journals via the Crossref REST API could therefore not run. The review instead used web search combined with the freely accessible, dated "recent" lists and article landing pages of the Copernicus journals — in particular Climate of the Past, Earth Surface Dynamics and the EGUsphere preprints. For every scored entry the short summary or abstract was consulted on the respective landing page.

What this means for coverage. The selection is shifted this week toward freely accessible titles because their abstracts are directly readable. The Elsevier (QSR, EPSL, GPC, P³, Catena, Geomorphology) and Wiley core journals (Boreas, JQS) could not be reliably captured with title and date within the window without Crossref; a title-only block without abstracts is therefore omitted rather than deriving results from incomplete hit lists. The dates given are journal publication dates (landing page), not Crossref created dates; the two usually differ by a few days. A direct East Africa / rift / AHP bullseye from an African primary study was not findable within the window; the profile is served this week through Quaternary palaeoceanography (MPT), monsoon/hydroclimate dynamics (Seoul) and the methods side (time-series transition detection).
