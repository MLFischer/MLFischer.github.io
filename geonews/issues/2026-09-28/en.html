In brief

More than two dozen scored works, with three must-reads this week — two abstract-backed, one based on title and journal. Leading is a Paleoceanography and Paleoclimatology study on the seasonal structure of the Pacific Walker Circulation during the Holocene: transient simulations separate orbital from greenhouse-gas forcing and show that orbital forcing shifts the zonal SST gradient in opposite seasonal directions (weaker in winter, stronger in summer), while anthropogenic greenhouse-gas increase since the mid-Holocene overprints this rhythm. Squarely within the profile: a Quaternary climate-dynamics question about separating natural and anthropogenic signals along a long forcing–response chain.

The second is highly connectable regionally: a Quaternary Research modelling study argues that the collapse of the "Green Sahara" drives Holocene cooling — precisely the African Humid Period land-surface feedback that sits at the centre of this profile and that standard climate models have so far missed. The third is a Quaternary Science Reviews paper on the multi-proxy palaeoenvironmental reconstruction of the earliest Oldowan at Nyayanga (western Kenya) — East African human–environment coupling in a rift context; because Elsevier provides no abstract, the assessment rests on title, journal and region and is flagged ⚠︎ title-based.

The block is methodologically broad: an Earth-system-model study of marine radiocarbon reservoir ages (cosmogenic ¹⁴C production vs. glacial–interglacial control), a Holocene moisture reconstruction from a Portuguese lake, the first identification of the Icelandic Vedde Ash in a Czech palaeolake, a ~100-kyr mercury record from the ICDP crater lake El'gygytgyn, a West African vegetation–climate study spanning 12,500 years, and a Bayesian daily-climate reconstruction for the Alps. Must-read (from 75)

1. Anthropogenic Disruption of Orbital-Scale Seasonal Asynchrony in the Tropical Pacific Walker Circulation During the Holocene · Paleoceanography and Paleoclimatology, 2026 · Score 85 A pair of transient Holocene simulations separates pure orbital forcing from orbital-plus-greenhouse-gas forcing to examine the seasonal architecture of the zonal sea-surface-temperature gradient (SSTG) in the equatorial Pacific. Finding per the abstract: pure orbital forcing produces a fundamental seasonal asymmetry — winter weakening but summer intensification of the SSTG — amplified by orbital modulation of the mixed-layer depth and the differential thermal sensitivity of the eastern Pacific cold tongue. Anthropogenic greenhouse-gas emissions since the mid-Holocene disrupt this natural rhythm: they warm the eastern Pacific across all seasons, counteracting the orbitally driven summer intensification while accelerating the winter decline. Squarely within the profile: Quaternary climate dynamics along a transient forcing–response chain, with a clean separation of natural and anthropogenic contributions — the same attribution question for long, non-linear system responses that is central to time-series and tipping-point analysis. Open access (AGU). Theme 36 · Method 20 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 26 Sep 2026) · DOI: 10.1029/2026pa005663

2. Green Sahara collapse drives Holocene cooling · Quaternary Research, 2026 · Score 84 Transient Holocene simulations with the fully coupled CESM1.2 (dynamic vegetation) plus an idealised "Green Sahara" experiment in which Early-to-Mid-Holocene desert soil properties in the Sahara are replaced with those from adjacent non-desert regions to mimic enhanced land-surface greening. Finding per the abstract: the Green Sahara experiment simulates a global mean annual temperature anomaly of 0.28 °C above pre-industrial, together with an approximately ninefold increase in annual precipitation over the Sahara — both consistent with proxy reconstructions and independent geological evidence. The authors argue that the widespread inability of climate models to reproduce the documented Holocene cooling (instead simulating unidirectional warming) is linked to the missing Green Sahara land-surface feedback: its collapse drives the cooling. Regionally and thematically at the profile's centre — African Humid Period and human–environment coupling, directly connectable to East African AHP and Chew Bahir questions. Theme 38 · Method 18 · Venue 8 · Connectivity 10 · Recency 10 (created 23 Sep 2026) · DOI: 10.1017/qua.2026.10116

Relevant (50–74)

4. Cosmogenic radiocarbon production and glacial cycles control marine radiocarbon archive variations · Communications Earth & Environment, 2026 · Score 74 An Earth-system model of intermediate complexity equipped with radiocarbon simulates marine reservoir ages over the past 50,000 years. Finding per the abstract: simulated reservoir ages range from 400 to 1,200 years at the sea surface and are systematically lower than previous uncoupled-model estimates — the result of stronger vertical mixing and higher air–sea CO₂ exchange rates. Short-term reservoir-age variations are dominated by changes in cosmogenic radiocarbon production, while glacial–interglacial climate variations act more slowly and are damped. The interhemispheric atmospheric radiocarbon gradient during the Laschamps excursion was, accordingly, up to twice as large as previously estimated. Connectable as a methodological chronology paper (Tier 2): reservoir-age corrections touch the same question as Bayesian age–depth and ¹⁴C calibration models. Open access (Nature portfolio). Theme 26 · Method 19 · Venue 12 · Connectivity 7 · Recency 10 (created 22 Sep 2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04073-y

5. North Atlantic Ocean–Atmosphere Dynamics Controlled the Hydroclimate in Western Iberia During the Holocene · Paleoceanography and Paleoclimatology, 2026 · Score 73 The first source-resolved reconstruction of centennial- to millennial-scale moisture variability over the last 11,000 years from Lake Peixão (Portugal), based on compound-specific hydrogen and carbon isotopes of plant-wax n-alkanes, supported by bulk organic geochemistry and continuous XRF elemental data. Finding per the abstract: a wet early Holocene, a progressive mid-Holocene drying trend culminating in maximum aridity around 4,000 calibrated years before present, and a transition to generally wetter but more variable late-Holocene conditions, partly overprinted by anthropogenic land use. The trends are interpreted as orbitally driven within North Atlantic ocean–atmosphere dynamics. Connectable as a mid-latitude palaeohydrological lake-sediment archive: the source-resolved moisture reconstruction touches the same methodological question as Central European water-balance and lake-sediment studies. Theme 26 · Method 18 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 22 Sep 2026) · DOI: 10.1029/2026pa005517

6. First finding of Icelandic tephra in Czechia: Vedde Ash identified in palaeolake sediments of Stará Jímka · Journal of Quaternary Science, 2026 · Score 72 First record of the Icelandic Vedde Ash (~12 ka) in the Bohemian Forest, identified in palaeolake sediments at Stará Jímka (Czechia) — the first Icelandic tephra layer in Czechia at all. Finding per the abstract: two discrete tephra horizons were found in two parallel lacustrine cores — the Laacher See Tephra (~13 ka BP) and, in both cores, the Vedde Ash (~12 ka BP), assigned via combined stratigraphic, chronological and geochemical evidence. These layers provide chronostratigraphic markers and a robust framework for correlating and dating Late Glacial sedimentary archives across Central and Northern Europe; the record also opens the possibility of an independent test of Greenland–Europe climate coupling. Connectable as Quaternary tephrochronology and a chronology tool for Central European lake sediments (Tier 2). Theme 27 · Method 19 · Venue 8 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 25 Sep 2026) · DOI: 10.1002/jqs.70120

7. Mercury cycling during the last glacial period in Arctic Lake El'gygytgyn, NE Russia · Journal of Quaternary Science, 2026 · Score 72 One of the longest (~100-kyr) lacustrine mercury-accumulation records in the Arctic, from the ICDP crater lake El'gygytgyn in remote north-eastern Russia. Finding per the abstract: despite the isolated setting, anomalously high background Hg values point to a local, non-atmospheric source — probably Hg migrating upward along faults beneath the lake floor (tectonically mobilised mercury). Centennial- to millennial-scale environmental signals are superimposed on this high background. The work shows that tectonic Hg fluxes can complicate the interpretation of sedimentary Hg records in tectonically active regions. Connectable as a long ICDP drill-core record (a core profile theme) and as a methodological caution for multi-proxy interpretation in rift basins, where tectonic overprinting is likewise to be expected. Theme 30 · Method 17 · Venue 8 · Connectivity 7 · Recency 10 (created 24 Sep 2026) · DOI: 10.1002/jqs.70118

8. Climate variability over the past 12,500 years has shaped vegetation dynamics differently across West African savannas and forests · The Holocene, 2026 · Score 72 Pollen-based compositional and functional analysis of vegetation dynamics across West African savannas and forests from the Younger Dryas to the present. Finding per the abstract: fossil pollen assemblages were grouped into ecological domains and harmonised with TRY trait records (including specific leaf area, wood density, seed mass); functional dispersion (FDis), squared chord distance and principal coordinate analysis were used to detect patterns. Sahelian and Sudanian savannas are compositionally distinct but only weakly differentiated functionally; functional dispersion increases significantly through time in the Sahel but not in Sudan. Forest assemblages show greater functional dispersion and compositional turnover than savannas. Connectable as an African vegetation–climate study across glacial–interglacial to Holocene timescales — methodologically transferable to East African pollen-based reconstructions and human–environment coupling in the rift. Theme 30 · Method 16 · Venue 8 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 25 Sep 2026) · DOI: 10.1177/09596836261487403

Climate and paleoseismicity co-recorded in a tectono-karstic Lake: a multi-proxy late Pleistocene–Holocene record from Lake Iffer (Middle Atlas, Morocco) · Palaeogeography, Palaeoclimatology, Palaeoecology, 2026 · Score 72 · ⚠︎ title-based Multi-proxy reconstruction from a tectono-karstic lake in the Middle Atlas (Morocco) that co-records climate and palaeoseismicity signals across the late Pleistocene and Holocene. Part C — Science policy (DE/EU)

WissZeitVG — amendment in parliamentary deliberation. Following the cabinet decision of 29 July 2026, the bill to modernise fixed-term employment law in academia is in parliamentary deliberation (Committee for Education, Research and Technology Assessment). At the core of the draft are, for the first time, binding minimum contract terms: three years for doctoral researchers' initial contracts, two years for postdocs; the maximum fixed-term duration in the postdoc phase is unified. For student assistants a minimum term of one year and a raised maximum fixed-term duration are foreseen. As orientation, not established fact: minimum terms improve the plannability of early contracts, while unifying the postdoc maximum may narrow individual carry-over flexibility; the further parliamentary path and timetable are open. Sources: BMFTR — Cabinet adopts WissZeitVG amendment · Forschung & Lehre — WissZeitVG focus.

2027 federal budget — parliamentary deliberation in autumn. The government draft has been in place since 6 July 2026. The BMFTR core budget stands at around €22 billion — a nominal increase of only about 0.7 percent over 2026. Real growth is almost exclusively in the special fund for infrastructure and climate neutrality, whose R&D investments rise markedly in 2027 relative to 2026. Bundestag readings run through the autumn. As orientation: the regular budget stagnates in real terms given current wage and material cost increases, keeping the operating situation of the funding organisations under pressure. Sources: Forschung & Lehre — Only a slim increase for the BMFTR core budget · Table.Briefings — 2027 budget.

DFG cuts at the postdoc level remain in force. Because of high wage increases and rising equipment and consumable costs that the promised annual funding increments do not cover, the DFG has postponed the funding start of new Research Training Group applications and suspended the approval of postdoc positions. For postdoc career paths in the geosciences this removes one funding avenue for now, increasing the pressure on individual-grant and EU funding. Source: Fakultätentag Informatik.
