Part A — Papers of the week

Covered are papers with a Crossref created date from 19 October 2026 onward that were not listed in a previous issue. For every entry with an accessible abstract (OpenAlex or Crossref), the assessment is based on that abstract; titles without an available abstract are marked ⚠︎ title-based.

Must-read (75+)

1. Late Quaternary lake filling and drying in northern Australia's largest freshwater lake · Journal of Quaternary Science, 2026 · Score 84 Lake Woods (Jurrkulu) fills today during strong Indo-Australian summer monsoons to a maximum depth of ~5 m; two arcuate ridges 20–40 m above the lake floor point to much higher past fillings. The authors combine 20 single-grain OSL ages, basin hypsometry, catchment-wide denudation rates and rare earth elements for provenance. Findings per abstract: two highstands at 195 ± 16 ka and 98 ± 7 ka (20 and 15 m above the lake floor) plus a third filling event (18.5 m) with a Bayesian-modelled boundary age of 95.9–53.8 ka (95% credible interval). They find no evidence for the previously reported late MIS 3 highstand; Holocene deposits do not allow robust quantification of filling episodes, and the lake floor shows strong post-depositional mixing (maximum ages < 143 ka). Denudation rates (0.83–2.05 m/Myr) are among the lowest in northern Australia, so the monsoon record is, according to the authors, only discontinuous — with exceptional episodic filling in MIS 5–4. Assessment: a case study for shoreline chronologies in shallow, episodically filled basins; methodologically transferable to reconstructing lake highstands in East African rift basins. Topic 38 · Method 20 · Venue 8 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 28 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1002/jqs.70130

2. Workshop report: Lake KINneret Drilling (KIND) – reconstructing Pliocene–Quaternary hydroclimate and seismic history of the Levant · Scientific Drilling, 2026 · Score 83 Workshop report (no new measurements). Lake Kinneret occupies a pull-apart basin on the Dead Sea Transform; seismic profiles and short cores show a thick, authigenic-calcite-dominated, largely stratigraphically ordered sequence since the late Miocene. Project aim per abstract: an exceptionally long Plio-Quaternary chronostratigraphic reference record with potentially sub-centennial resolution for hydroclimate, palaeoseismicity, geomagnetism and palaeobotany — as a building block of a meridional synthesis of continental and marine core records along the East African Rift – Dead Sea Transform corridor. The region is also a main corridor of out-of-Africa dispersal. Assessment: directly connected to existing rift-lake drilling in East Africa and relevant for planning comparative syntheses along the corridor. Drilling status and funding are not stated in the abstract. Topic 40 · Method 16 · Venue 7 · Connectivity 10 · Recency 10 (created 29 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.5194/sd-35-255-2026

3. Holocene millennial climate variability influenced by tropical–Antarctic ocean–atmosphere interactions · PNAS, 2026 · Score 81 A transient Holocene simulation with EC-Earth (8–0 ka) plus a global compilation of 304 temperature reconstructions identify, per abstract, a robust, self-sustained ~1350-year oscillatory mode. Within each cycle, Antarctic Bottom Water (AABW) strengthens with developing warming and an El Niño-like tropical SST response; positive feedbacks (sea-ice albedo, tropical air–sea interaction, SST–AABW) amplify the warming until reduced brine rejection from sea-ice retreat weakens AABW and initiates the cooling phase. The dominant period matches proxy-based estimates and is confirmed by further simulations. The authors propose this as an alternative to solar or deep-ocean explanations (interpretation; a model result, not direct proxy evidence of the mechanism). Relevant for spectral and phase analyses of Holocene proxy series, where millennial cycles are often attributed to solar forcing. Topic 30 · Method 20 · Venue 15 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 27 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2538010123

4. Non-analog environments supported early Homo sapiens in southern Africa 200 000 years ago · Communications Earth & Environment, 2026 · Score 80 A plant-wax n-alkane isotope record for the sites Florisbad and Lombard's Drift (South Africa), anchored by new luminescence ages that revise the age of the Florisbad cranium (the revised value is not given in the abstract). Findings per abstract: contrasting, non-analog plant environments during the Middle and Late Pleistocene, supported by multiple lines of evidence. The authors see this as a basis for evaluating the cultural adaptation strategies of early Homo sapiens through time. Assessment: thematically close to human–environment questions in East African archives; "non-analog" is a caution against modern transfer functions. Topic 36 · Method 15 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 30 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04116-4

5. Morphological variation in Middle-Late Pleistocene stone points: a pan-African perspective · Quaternary Science Reviews, 2026 · Score 79 The first continent-wide geometric morphometric analysis (elliptic Fourier analysis, multivariate statistics) of almost 1000 points from 44 assemblages in eastern, northern and southern Africa. Findings per abstract: shape is dominated by two axes (elongation vs. breadth; basal tanging). Modelled palaeoenvironment (mainly annual precipitation and temperature seasonality) and region are the strongest predictors; only the North African Aterian forms a consistently distinct group, while Howiesons Poort and Still Bay points (smaller sample) fall largely within "generic" MSA variability. Raw material acts as an additional constraint. Assessment: a quantitative link between artefact variability and palaeoclimate — its robustness depends on the quality of the modelled palaeoenvironmental fields. Topic 34 · Method 16 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 29 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1016/j.quascirev.2026.110314

Relevant (50–74)

6. Seasonal Modulation of ENSO Teleconnections to Horn of Africa Rainfall · Geophysical Research Letters, 2026 · Score 74 Nonlinear interactions between ENSO and the semiannual and annual ITCZ migration generate, per abstract, a combination mode (C-Mode) with significant spectral peaks near 1 and 2 yr⁻¹ that modulates timing and amplitude of both rainy seasons (OND, MAM) over the Horn of Africa; including it substantially improves the representation of the bimodal rainfall response. Instrumental timescale, but methodologically close to combination tones in proxy spectra — and relevant for interpreting rainfall seasonality in the southern Ethiopian Rift. Topic 25 · Method 20 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 30 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1029/2026gl123196

7. Geochemical and granulometric fingerprints of 8200 years of westerly wind variability in inner-fjord lake sediments from Svalbard · Boreas, 2026 · Score 72 Laminated sediments from a closed-basin lake on Spitsbergen; geochemical, visual and grain-size fingerprints within a multiproxy geostatistical framework link aeolian input to specific source areas (Fe-rich coarse silt from upwind dolerites). Findings per abstract: four phases of enhanced aeolian activity in the Mid and Late Holocene; shifts in sources coincide with the end of the Holocene Thermal Maximum and the onset of the Neoglacial, but the wind signal itself remains comparatively stable over the long term, with no clear correspondence to regional palaeoclimate records. Methodologically transferable to provenance analysis of aeolian fractions in lake sediments. Topic 30 · Method 18 · Venue 8 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 26 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1111/bor.70089

8. Science Needs for a Terrestrial Biosphere Tipping Point Early Warning System · Reviews of Geophysics, 2026 · Score 72 Review: how can Earth-observation data be used for early-warning systems of biosphere tipping points? A synthesis of potential tipping elements and mathematical methods for resilience estimation, emphasising spatio-temporal resilience indicators that exploit short but spatially dense EO series, including space-for-time substitution and parallel forcing data. No palaeodata; conceptually connected to early-warning analyses of proxy series, which face the opposite problem (long but spatially sparse) (assessment). Topic 30 · Method 18 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 3 · Recency 10 (created 27 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1029/2025rg000900

9. Anchoring Anatolian tree-rings and Mesopotamian chronology with single-year radiocarbon patterns and ice-core evidence · PNAS, 2026 · Score 71 Single-year ¹⁴C patterns in Anatolian timbers anchor a combined tree-ring chronology for 2072–744 BCE; together with archaeological and high-resolution ice-core evidence, the remaining dating uncertainty supports, per abstract, the middle–low chronology for Mesopotamia (2000–1500 BCE). It also yields an annually resolved palaeoclimate record. Methodologically relevant for wiggle-matching and coupling independent chronologies. Topic 25 · Method 18 · Venue 15 · Connectivity 3 · Recency 10 (created 26 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2615871123

10. Monsoon zonal seesaw: Development of the South Asian monsoon aridified North Africa in the Cenozoic · Science Advances, 2026 · Score 69 Palaeoclimate ensembles plus a model of the atmospheric response to imposed diabatic heating: per abstract, early Cenozoic North African aridification was primarily driven by the intensification (and poleward shift) of the South Asian monsoon via the monsoon–desert mechanism (large-scale subsidence over North Africa), rather than being an independent response to palaeogeography. The authors identify a persistent dynamical pathway between the two monsoon systems whose expression at other times depended on competing forcings. Pre-Quaternary, but relevant as a dynamical framework for Africa–Asia coupling on orbital timescales as well (assessment). Topic 25 · Method 13 · Venue 15 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 30 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aee1112
