What is GeoNews? An automatically generated weekly digest. A research workflow scans the publications of the past one to two weeks along with funding, policy and community sources, scores the hits against a fixed set of criteria, and assembles this issue from them.

The thematic profile is aligned with the research interests of Markus L. Selection, scoring and short texts are produced by machine and reviewed before publication. Details may contain errors; in case of doubt the linked original source prevails.

Each paper receives a score from 0–100 across five criteria: thematic proximity (40), methodological proximity (25), impact/venue (15), connectivity (10) and recency (10). Categories: from 75 must-read · 50–74 relevant · 30–49 radar. The score measures proximity to this newsletter's profile and prioritises reading — it is not a quality judgement on the individual paper.

Must-read (from 75)

1. Holocene hydroclimatic variability and multi-frequency analyses at Lake Sidi Ali (Morocco) · Climate of the Past, 2026 · Score 93 A Holocene sediment record from Lake Sidi Ali in the semi-arid to sub-humid Middle Atlas — one of the most climate-sensitive stretches of the North African desert margin. The chronology rests on ²¹⁰Pb/¹³⁷Cs and a ¹⁴C age on pollen concentrates; a high-resolution core-scanning XRF profile separates lake-internal (chemical precipitation) from lake-external (detrital input) processes. Redfit and wavelet analyses reveal distinct millennial- to centennial-scale periodicities, grouped via correlation analysis of highly significant spectra into three "Redfit Proxy Groups" (RPGs). Result: two partly decoupled regimes — RPG 1 (Ca, Sr, Ca/Ti, Sr/Ti) with 2- and 1-kyr periodicities, interpreted as a precipitation/evaporation signal in the context of North Atlantic and solar forcing, and RPG 2 (Fe, Ti, K, Si/Ti) with 3.5- and 1.5-kyr periodicities, interpreted via lake productivity and detrital input. Connective as a core Tier-1 theme (Holocene hydroclimate and water-balance reconstruction at the desert margin) and methodologically as a clean example of spectral time-series decomposition on a lacustrine multiproxy archive — the same Redfit/wavelet toolkit and the same question of separable forcing regimes arise directly at East African rift lakes. Theme 40 · Method 22 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 10 · Recency 10 (publ. 10 Aug 2026) · DOI: 10.5194/cp-22-1537-2026

Relevant (50–74)

2. Early to Middle Holocene aridification trend in southeastern Iberia and the termination of the last North African Humid Period revealed by a multi-proxy speleothem record · EGUsphere (preprint, under review for Climate of the Past), 2026 · Score 73 A multi-proxy stalagmite from Simarrón II cave (south-eastern Iberia), 9.7–4.4 ka BP, combining calcite δ¹⁸O/δ¹³C, Mg/Ca, petrography, fluid-inclusion isotopes (δ¹⁸O_fi, δ²H) and TEX₈₆ palaeotemperatures. Result: between 9.7 and 6.0 ka, low δ¹⁸O_carb, δ¹³C and Mg/Ca values indicate relatively wet conditions; from ~6.0 ka the proxies rise progressively and stabilise after ~5.5 ka — a shift to arid conditions within about 500 years, which the authors link to the termination of the last North African Humid Period. TEX₈₆ temperatures (13–17 °C) exceed the present-day cave value (11.7 °C). Connective as a Tier-1 theme (AHP termination and its pace): the same methodological question about the tempo and spatial coherence of the wet-to-dry transition that is central in East African archives — here through a European speleothem rather than an African lake sediment, and therefore with thematic rather than regional connectivity. Preprint, hence the low venue weight; the finding stands provisionally pending completion of review. Theme 40 · Method 17 · Venue 4 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 6 (publ. 7 Aug 2026) · DOI: 10.5194/egusphere-2026-4435

3. Human climatic and ecological drivers of Holocene fire regimes in North America · Communications Earth & Environment, 2026 · Score 69 A synthesis of 140 Holocene-length (12 kyr) charcoal records via a systematic database search with subsequent sensitivity analysis; ecoregion-scale spatiotemporal variations are compared with reconstructed climate parameters, global CO₂ emissions, ecological productivity and human landscape impacts, complemented by a taphonomically corrected comparison with archaeological data. Result: temperature, emissions, vegetation and human impacts each drove Holocene fire activity; the authors date the onset of human influence on the fire regime to about 6,500 cal yr BP — earlier than previous estimates. Connective as a multiproxy palaeoecology study on human–environment coupling: separating climatic from anthropogenic signals in a noisy proxy (charcoal) across many sites is methodologically related to the attribution question in continental palaeoenvironmental archives. The high venue weight (Nature family) carries the score at only moderate thematic proximity. Theme 25 · Method 15 · Venue 13 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (publ. 12 Aug 2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-03918-w

4. The influence of glacial Northern Hemisphere ice sheets on atmospheric circulation · Climate of the Past, 2026 · Score 64 A modelling study of the effect of the glacial Northern Hemisphere ice sheets on global atmospheric circulation. Result: the ice sheets shifted the North Atlantic winds southward and increased summer rainfall across Eurasia; the height and shape of the ice sheets had a much greater influence on global wind and rainfall patterns than changes in Earth's orbit, greenhouse gases or surface albedo. Connective as a Tier-2 climate-dynamics study: the question of which boundary condition (ice-mass topography vs orbital/radiative forcing) to attribute the rainfall-relevant circulation change to touches the forcing decomposition that is also central to glacial African monsoon and water-balance dynamics. Theme 25 · Method 16 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 6 (publ. 5 Aug 2026) · DOI: 10.5194/cp-22-1457-2026

5. Multidecadal behavior of the North Atlantic Oscillation during the last millennium · Climate of the Past, 2026 · Score 63 A new reconstruction of the North Atlantic Oscillation (NAO) over the last millennium from water-isotope proxy data from ice cores, speleothems, tree rings and other archives. Result: pronounced but irregular multidecadal to centennial variability; the authors stress that better representation of this low-frequency variability in climate models is necessary for robust projections. Connective as a Tier-2 study reconstructing a mode of variability from a multi-archive isotope network — methodologically related to the question of how robust low-frequency signals are to archive and calibration choices, which also holds for continental hydroclimate series. Theme 25 · Method 15 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 6 (publ. 4 Aug 2026) · DOI: 10.5194/cp-22-1423-2026

6. A cold-season temperature reconstruction for central Japan since 259 CE from a two-millennia Kiso Chamaecyparis ring-width chronology · EGUsphere (preprint, under review for Climate of the Past), 2026 · Score 52 A near-1,800-year reconstruction of November-to-April temperature in central Japan combining tree rings from living trees, archaeological wood, historic buildings and buried wood. The series most reliably captures interannual to decadal variability, identifies sustained warmth from 1999 to 2015, and remains more uncertain in longer-term variations. Connective as a geochronological-dendroclimatological case study (chronology building from heterogeneous wood sources, calibration, uncertainty estimation) at the edge of the profile; preprint, hence the low venue weight. Theme 25 · Method 14 · Venue 4 · Connectivity 3 · Recency 6 (publ. 7 Aug 2026) · DOI: 10.5194/egusphere-2026-4506

Part C — Science policy (DE/EU)

WissZeitVG — amendment adopted by the cabinet on 29 Jul 2026, Bundestag to follow in the autumn. The federal cabinet adopted the bill to modernise fixed-term employment law in science on 29 July 2026; the May draft was revised in parts after criticism from associations. At its core are, for the first time, binding minimum contract durations: three years for doctoral researchers' first contracts, two years for postdocs; the maximum fixed-term duration in the postdoc phase is standardised at six years. For student research assistants the draft provides a minimum term of one year and raises the maximum fixed-term duration from six to eight years. According to parliamentary sources, the Bundestag debate is to be expected after the summer recess, i.e. in September. As interpretation, not established fact: minimum durations improve the predictability of early contracts, while the removal of earlier carry-over flexibility further narrows individual fixed-term planning in the postdoc phase — the further parliamentary path remains to be watched. Sources: BMFTR — Cabinet adopts amendment to the WissZeitVG · Bundestag — Reform of the Wissenschaftszeitvertragsgesetz · Wiarda blog — WissZeitVG: BMFTR presents draft.

Federal budget 2027 — cabinet decision of 6 Jul 2026, Bundestag to follow in the autumn. The government draft has stood since 6 July 2026. The BMFTR core budget is about EUR 22 billion — a nominal increase of only about 0.7 percent over 2026. Real increases are almost exclusively in the special fund for infrastructure and climate neutrality, whose R&D investments rise markedly in 2027 over 2026. Sources: Forschung & Lehre — Only a slim increase for the BMFTR core budget · Table.Briefings — Budget 2027.

DFG cuts at the postdoc level remain in force. Owing to high pay rises and increased equipment and consumables costs that the promised annual funding increments do not cover, the DFG has postponed the funding start of new Research Training Group applications and suspended the approval of postdoc positions. For postdoc career paths in the geosciences this removes a funding route for now, increasing the pressure on individual-grant and EU funds. Source: Fakultätentag Informatik.

Methods & sources

The paper review follows the fixed set of criteria (thematic proximity, methodological proximity, impact/venue, connectivity, recency) and excludes DOIs already listed in an earlier issue. Funding opportunities, science policy and dates rely on the sources linked in Parts B to D.

On how this issue came about — a different research path. Crossref access from the compute environment was blocked as in previous weeks; in addition, the browser-based fetch interface otherwise used as a fallback was unavailable this week. The systematic ISSN query of the core journals via the Crossref REST API could therefore not run. The review instead used web search combined with the freely accessible "recent" lists and article landing pages of the open-access journals — in particular the Copernicus layer (Climate of the Past, EGUsphere preprints), Nature open access (Communications Earth & Environment) and MDPI. For every scored entry the abstract was consulted on the respective landing page.

What this means for coverage. The selection is shifted this week toward freely accessible titles because their abstracts are directly readable. The Elsevier (QSR, EPSL, GPC, P³, Boreas as Wiley, Catena, Geomorphology) and Wiley core journals could not be reliably captured with title and date within the window without Crossref; a title-only block without abstracts is therefore omitted rather than deriving results from incomplete hit lists. The dates given are journal publication dates (landing page), not Crossref created dates; the two usually differ by a few days. A direct East Africa / rift / AHP bullseye from an African primary study was not findable within the window; the profile is served through the North African desert margin (Sidi Ali, Simarrón II) and the methods side.
