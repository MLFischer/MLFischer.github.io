In brief

Around three dozen papers scored, with five must-reads this week — four supported by abstracts, one based on title and journal only. At the top is a direct primary hit from the core of the profile: a Quaternary Science Reviews study on the ⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr record of palaeolake Chew Bahir (southern Ethiopian Rift, past ~40 ka). According to the abstract, local water balance (potassium proxy, close to precession) and water provenance (Sr isotopes, more closely tied to glacial–interglacial boundary conditions) decouple from about 9.8 ka onwards — the connection to the Ethiopian Highlands persists while the lowland balance turns negative.

Must-read (75+)

1. Decoupled hydroclimate dynamics in eastern Africa as revealed by the Sr isotope record of paleolake Chew Bahir · Quaternary Science Reviews, 2026 · Score 93 ⁸⁷Sr/⁸⁶Sr as a tracer of water provenance and hydrological connectivity in the Chew Bahir basin (southern Ethiopian Rift), measured on biogenic and authigenic material from multiple cores spanning the past ~40 ka. Findings according to the abstract: the Sr signal reflects basin-wide lake-water composition largely independent of substrate and captures contributions from the Ethiopian Highlands. Until the onset of the Holocene, provenance (Sr) and local hydrology (K content) vary similarly; from about 9.8 ka they diverge: potassium indicates increasing aridity (especially after ~6 ka), while Sr indicates continued connectivity with the highlands despite an increasingly negative local precipitation–evaporation balance. A Sr mass-balance sensitivity analysis shows that water quantity alone cannot explain the lowest Holocene values — a shift in provenance and/or source-water composition is required. The authors link the local balance to precession and the Sr signal more to glacial–interglacial boundary conditions. Core of the profile: Chew Bahir, the African Humid Period, water balance — and a methodological template for modelling provenance and balance proxies jointly. Topic 40 · Method 22 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 10 · Recency 10 (created 05.10.2026) · DOI: 10.1016/j.quascirev.2026.110308

2. Strong latitudinal contrasts in global wet/dry patterns since the Last Glacial Maximum and under future warming · Communications Earth & Environment, 2026 · Score 82 Global climate simulations combined with 191 palaeoclimate records (including lake-level records) on terrestrial moisture change over the past 21,000 years. Findings according to the abstract: progressive wetting at high northern latitudes from the LGM to the pre-industrial; mid-latitude regions dominated by westerlies are wetter during cold periods, low-latitude monsoon regions during warm periods. Causes given are seasonal changes in precipitation and evaporation and circulation shifts driven by ice sheets, greenhouse gases and insolation; a similar latitude-dependent structure is reported in modern observations. Future simulations project drying across many subtropical and mid-latitude regions. Relevant as a framework for East African lake-level and AHP records ("monsoon regions wet in warm phases") and as a lake-level-based data–model comparison. Open access. Topic 38 · Method 18 · Venue 12 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 6 (created 03.10.2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-04078-7

3. A hybrid DeepGR4J and regionalization framework for lake water balance assessment in data-scarce regions: Application to Lake Tana, Ethiopia · Journal of Hydrology: Regional Studies, 2026 · Score 77 Present-day water balance of Lake Tana (Ethiopian Highlands, source of the Blue Nile) for 2000–2020: the conceptual GR4J rainfall–runoff model with LSTM-based error correction (DeepGR4J), combined with regression-based regionalisation for ungauged sub-catchments (CHIRPS, ERA5, Hargreaves evapotranspiration). Findings according to the abstract: mean inflow ~12.6 km³/yr, outflow term ~12.7 km³/yr, net storage decline ~0.2 km³/yr; observed lake levels are reproduced well according to the authors (NSE ~0.94), and ungauged catchments contribute substantially. Not a palaeo paper — but the same methodological question as palaeo lake-level modelling of East African rift lakes: how to close a water balance with incomplete input data. Transferable as a calibration and comparison framework. Topic 34 · Method 18 · Venue 7 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 07.10.2026) · DOI: 10.1016/j.ejrh.2026.104046

4. Two millennia of Caspian Sea level change and Hyrcanian forest dynamics: A multi-proxy record from the Anzali Lagoon, northern Iran · Quaternary Science Reviews, 2026 · Score 76 Multi-proxy analysis of a 5-m core from the Anzali Lagoon (SW Caspian coast): Caspian Sea level and Hyrcanian forest vegetation over ~2000 years. Findings according to the abstract: highstands (including Parthian–early Sasanian, early and late Little Ice Age) are marked by Caspian indicator organisms (dinocysts, foraminifera, bivalves) and suggest breaching of the Anzali spit; lowstands (Sasanian–early Medieval, late Medieval) show lagoon isolation and freshwater influence. The late Medieval warm/dry phase strongly affected vegetation; two episodes of probable anthropogenic deforestation are identified. Relevant as a lake-level archive of a large closed-basin lake with a human-environment signal — structurally comparable to rift-lake balances. Part C — Science policy (DE/EU)

WissZeitVG — Bundesrat has issued its opinion. In its first reading on 25 Sep 2026 the Bundesrat (upper house of the German parliament) commented on the amendment to the Academic Fixed-Term Contract Act (printed paper 456/26 B). Only one demand was adopted: if the minimum contract duration is undercut without justification, the contract should count as fixed-term for the statutory minimum — not as permanent. Further committee recommendations (including the finance committee's concerns about lost budget flexibility and the social-affairs committee's call for binding post-PhD career perspectives) did not find a majority. Next steps: the federal government's response, then deliberation in the Bundestag. The core of the draft remains minimum contract durations (three years for doctoral researchers' first contracts, two years for postdocs). Assessment: for third-party projects with shorter durations, the question of bridging finance remains practically relevant. Source: Forschung & Lehre — Bundesrat fordert Nachbesserung bei BAföG- und WissZeitVG.

Methods & sources

Paper screening follows the fixed set of criteria (topical fit, methodological fit, impact/venue, connectivity, recency) and excludes DOIs already listed in an earlier issue. Funding opportunities, science policy and dates are based on the sources linked in Parts B to D.

How this issue was produced. Direct network access from the computing environment was blocked. The Crossref REST API was queried via the integrated browser interface (fetch()): ISSN queries for the core journals (QSR, Climate of the Past, Quaternary Science Advances, GPC, EPSL, Boreas, JQS, Quaternary Research, P³, Nature Communications, Nature Geoscience, Communications Earth & Environment) with from-created-date:2026-09-28 and created sorting, complemented by topic queries (paleoclimate, Quaternary lake sediment, East African Rift/Turkana, African Humid Period, paleoflood fluvial, Bayesian age-depth chronology, early-warning tipping point, lake level water balance, hominin paleoenvironment, ICDP drilling, tephra chronology, LGM and others). Abstracts for the candidates were then retrieved via the OpenAlex API (reconstructed from the abstract_inverted_index) and from individual publisher pages (Nature Portfolio, Copernicus).

What this means for coverage. For the first time, most Elsevier titles are assessed on the basis of abstracts. Where neither Crossref nor OpenAlex holds an abstract, the entry is marked ⚠︎ title-based; no findings beyond the title were invented. Dates given are Crossref created dates (registration), which may differ from the online publication date by a few days. Crossref topic queries return a lot of off-topic noise; pre-selection was done via keywords and then manually, so individual relevant papers outside the core journals may be missing.
