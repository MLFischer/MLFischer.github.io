What is GeoNews? An automatically generated weekly digest. A research workflow screens the publications of the past one to two weeks along with funding, policy and community sources, scores the hits against a fixed set of criteria, and assembles this issue from the result.

The thematic profile is aligned with the research interests of Markus L. Fischer (Palaeoclimate Dynamics group, University of Potsdam): Quaternary palaeoclimate and palaeoenvironmental reconstruction, the East African Rift, the African Humid Period and human–environment coupling, lake-level and water-balance modelling, palaeofloods and fluvial geomorphology, ICDP drill cores, and linear and nonlinear time-series analysis. Anyone working in these fields will probably find something useful here — it is explicitly not a complete survey of Quaternary science.

Selection, scoring and summaries are produced by machine and reviewed before publication. Details may contain errors; where in doubt, the linked original source governs.

Each paper receives a score from 0 to 100 across five criteria: thematic fit (40), methodological fit (25), impact/venue (15), applicability (10) and recency (10). Categories: 75 and above, must-read · 50–74, relevant · 30–49, on the radar. The score measures proximity to this newsletter's profile and sets reading priority; it is not a quality judgement on the individual paper.

Relevant (50–74)

1. Shrub-mediated vertical coupling regulates Holocene ecosystem variability and transition dynamics · Communications Earth & Environment, 2026 · Score 74 A multiproxy analysis (pollen, charcoal, palaeoclimate and human-activity proxies) from the Eastern China Monsoon Region across the Holocene, examining how vertical vegetation structure — tree, shrub and herb layers — regulates ecosystem variability and potential tipping points. Central finding: in northern temperate forest–grassland mosaics and southern subtropical forests, shrubs are associated with reduced variability — consistent with a buffering that maintains continuity between canopy and ground layers. In the more open, warm-temperate central region this buffering is weak, and lower variability there depends mainly on a closed woody framework; potential tipping points are asynchronous, with earlier transitions in the structurally weaker central region. Doubly relevant here: the theme of ecosystem variability and tipping points in the Holocene on one hand, and methodologically the identification of transitions and early-warning signatures in multiproxy time series on the other — the same question addressed for the AHP termination using tipping-point and early-warning methods. Theme 25 · Method 20 · Venue 11 · Applicability 8 · Recency 10 (created 29 Jul 2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-03877-2

2. Decoupling climate and volcanic-seismic signals from the periglacial Jurasee Lake at King George Island (South Shetland Islands, Antarctica) for the Middle-to-Late Holocene · The Holocene, 2026 · Score 71 A sediment core from the periglacial Jurasee Lake on King George Island, spanning the middle to late Holocene (890 ± 30 to 4870 ± 50 cal a BP). Volcanic-seismic processes on Deception Island — up to a caldera collapse on the scale of the largest of the past several millennia — are strongly expressed in the region. The work combines XRF scanning, X-ray diffraction, magnetic susceptibility and sedimentation rates to separate the volcanic-seismic inputs from the climatic-sedimentary signal component in the lake sediment. Methodologically the most interesting paper of the week for this profile: decoupling non-climatic event signals from the underlying climatic signature in a lake core is the same core question posed for rift lake sediments and in palaeoflood research — how to cleanly resolve superimposed signal sources in a single archive. Theme 25 · Method 20 · Venue 8 · Applicability 8 · Recency 10 (created 29 Jul 2026) · DOI: 10.1177/09596836261468297

3. Patterns and seasonality in the mid-Holocene hydroclimate of the Thar Desert in northwestern India · Communications Earth & Environment, 2026 · Score 69 A model-based investigation of the seasonality of mid-Holocene precipitation in the Thar Desert (northwest India) using the multi-model simulations of PMIP4, linked to the question of spatial moisture distribution and its coupling to the Indus cultural transformation. Finding: the models agree with proxy data on a strengthened summer monsoon across the Thar in the mid-Holocene, with the north receiving about double the effective moisture of the south — synchronous with the spread of agriculture alongside continued foraging. Stronger winter-rain changes in the south explain, per the paper, part of the spatial heterogeneity. Relevant as a clean example of model–data integration on monsoon seasonality and of human–environment coupling at an arid monsoon margin — conceptually transferable to East African monsoon and AHP contexts. Theme 25 · Method 15 · Venue 11 · Applicability 8 · Recency 10 (created 28 Jul 2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s43247-026-03857-6

4. A multiproxy approach to address signals of MIS 2 climate fluctuations using loess–palaeosol sequences along a slope transect at Langenlois (lower Austria) · Quaternary Research, 2026 · Score 69 Loess–palaeosol sequences at three closely spaced sites along a slope transect near Langenlois (Lower Austria) across Marine Isotope Stage 2 (MIS 2). A stated objective is to disentangle the pedosedimentary influence of local slope processes from regional to global climate change, and thereby assess the reconstruction potential of the profiles. High-resolution colour, grain-size and geochemical analyses capture local depositional dynamics and identify weak pedogenic signals that are macroscopically hard to distinguish in the field; luminescence dating and relative stratigraphy provide the chronological framework. The results indicate, among other things, a pronounced landscape transition out of tundra conditions. Relevant as a Central European Quaternary archive with a clean separation of local and higher-order signals — precisely the methodological question that is also central to interpreting lake-sediment and palaeoflood archives. Theme 25 · Method 18 · Venue 8 · Applicability 8 · Recency 10 (created 29 Jul 2026) · DOI: 10.1017/qua.2026.10097

5. Sedimentary archives of Mediterranean climate variability: grain-size signatures in Halk El Menjel Lagoon during the late Holocene · The Holocene, 2026 · Score 67 A late-Holocene sedimentary archive (HK2) from Halk El Menjel Lagoon in the south-central Mediterranean (Tunisia), spanning about 4400 years. The chronology rests on three new AMS radiocarbon dates integrated into a Bayesian age–depth model (rBchron). Grain-size analysis of 750 samples identifies twelve subpopulations, grouped by principal component and distribution analysis into three transport modes — aeolian (600–1000 µm), fluvial (250–580 µm) and hydraulic runoff. Relevant less for the region than for the methodology: Bayesian age–depth modelling plus grain-size end-member separation of transport processes is exactly the toolbox applied to lake sediments. Theme 25 · Method 20 · Venue 8 · Applicability 8 · Recency 6 (created 21 Jul 2026) · DOI: 10.1177/09596836261450777

6. Subfossil Coleoptera as palaeoecological indicators of environmental conditions during the Neolithic: Serteya II site, NW Russia · The Holocene, 2026 · Score 65 The first reconstruction of shore-zone evolution of the Great Serteya Palaeolake Basin (Western Dvina Lakeland, NW Russia) based on subfossil Coleoptera (beetles), embedded in long-running geoarchaeological study of a Neolithic lakeshore settlement. From the beetle assemblages the authors infer marsh and fully aquatic phases as well as periods of highly variable lake level for the interval around 5.45–3.78 ka cal BP, coupled to climatically driven water-level fluctuations and the hydrological regime of the Serteyka River. Relevant as a proxy-based reconstruction of lake-level and shore dynamics in the context of prehistoric settlement — the same coupling of water balance and human–environment relations that is at issue in East African lake contexts. Theme 25 · Method 14 · Venue 8 · Applicability 8 · Recency 10 (created 29 Jul 2026) · DOI: 10.1177/09596836261450819

7. Extreme Events Reshape Flood Hazards Through Sediment-Induced Channel Change in Southern Taiwan · Geophysical Research Letters, 2026 · Score 63 An investigation of how extreme precipitation events alter flood hazards over the long term via sediment-induced channel change, using Typhoon Morakot (2009) across four catchments in southern Taiwan. Morakot triggered over 17,000 landslides, delivering large volumes of sediment to steep mountain rivers and reducing channel conveyance. Pre- and post-event digital elevation models were built from aerial imagery and Structure-from-Motion photogrammetry, and inundation was simulated with HEC-RAS 2D. Result: sediment-driven channel infilling increased inundation area by up to 44 percent, while reaches with little sediment supply showed thalweg erosion and reduced inundation. A recent event, but methodologically directly relevant to palaeoflood research: sediment supply modulates flood impact — a process analogue for interpreting fluvial extreme-event archives. Theme 25 · Method 15 · Venue 11 · Applicability 6 · Recency 6 (created 25 Jul 2026) · DOI: 10.1029/2026GL122788

8. Dispersal, source and glass chemistry of a third tephra unit within Iceland's Settlement Layer · Boreas, 2026 · Score 63 A description of a previously undocumented, dark-grey tephra unit within the Icelandic Settlement Layer (AD ~877), one of Iceland's most important isochron markers. Electron-probe micro-analyses identify the unit as a bimodal transitional alkali basalt; it was deposited less than a year before the Vatnaöldur unit. Relevant as a tephrochronology paper: precisely characterised ash layers are isochronous time markers that provide exactly the framework on which age–depth models in lake-sediment and soil archives are anchored. Theme 25 · Method 14 · Venue 8 · Applicability 6 · Recency 10 (created 26 Jul 2026) · DOI: 10.1111/bor.70079

9. Holocene sea-surface temperature as a regional driver of environmental shifts and its effects on humans of La Paz Bay, Gulf of California · The Holocene, 2026 · Score 62 A reconstruction of Holocene summer sea-surface temperatures (SST) of La Paz Bay (Baja California Sur, Mexico) from δ¹⁸O in shells of Pinctada mazatlanica, obtained from a ¹⁴C-dated Holocene coastal camp. Over the last ca. 8.4 ka the δ¹⁸O values vary; individual time slices (including 6936 and 2093 cal a BP) indicate markedly colder SST, and periods of low SST coincide with arid, periods of high SST with more humid conditions — with reference to the local human communities. Relevant as a shell-based isotope SST reconstruction with coupled SST–aridity–human relations. Theme 25 · Method 13 · Venue 8 · Applicability 6 · Recency 10 (created 31 Jul 2026) · DOI: 10.1177/09596836261468260

Part C — Research policy (Germany/EU)

WissZeitVG — reform adopted by cabinet on 29 July 2026. New since the previous week: the federal cabinet adopted the bill to modernise fixed-term employment law in science on 29 July 2026; the May ministry draft was revised in part after criticism from scientific associations. Its core is, for the first time, binding minimum contract durations: three years for first contracts of doctoral researchers, two years for postdocs; the maximum fixed-term duration in the postdoc phase is standardised at six years. The bill does not require Bundesrat approval; dates for the deliberations in the Bundestag and Bundesrat are not yet set. As interpretation rather than fact: minimum durations improve the predictability of early contracts, while the removal of earlier carry-over provisions further narrows individual fixed-term planning in the postdoc phase — anyone carrying unused time should watch the further parliamentary path. Sources: BMFTR — cabinet adopts amendment to the WissZeitVG · Federal Government — better working conditions in science · Forschung & Lehre — government takes up associations' criticism · Wiarda blog — decided, but provisional.

Federal budget 2027 — cabinet decision of 6 July 2026, Bundestag to follow in autumn. The government draft has stood since 6 July 2026. The research ministry's (BMFTR) core budget stands at around €22 billion — a nominal increase of only about 0.7 percent over 2026. Real increases come almost exclusively from the special fund for infrastructure and climate neutrality, whose R&D investments rise by roughly 60 percent in 2027 over 2026. The parliamentary deliberation (first reading) is due in autumn. As interpretation: the regular budget stagnates in real terms given current pay and materials cost increases, keeping the funding organisations under operational pressure. Sources: Forschung & Lehre — only a slim increase for the BMFTR core budget · Table.Briefings — Budget 2027.

DFG cuts at the postdoc level. Facing high collective-agreement pay rises and increased equipment and consumables costs that the guaranteed annual funding increments do not cover, the DFG has postponed the funding start for new Research Training Group applications and suspended approval of postdoc positions. For postdoc career paths in the geosciences this removes one funding route for the time being, increasing pressure on individual grants and EU money. Source: Fakultätentag Informatik.

Method & sources

Paper screening uses the Crossref REST API with topic- and ISSN-based queries and the from-created-date filter. DOIs already listed in an earlier issue are not repeated. Funding, research policy and deadlines rest on the sources linked in Parts B to D.

How this issue came about. Crossref access from the compute environment was blocked by a proxy 403, as in the previous week. The queries therefore ran entirely through the browser-based fetch interface — topic- and ISSN-based across the core journals (QSR, EPSL, GPC, P³, Boreas, JQS, Quaternary Research, Communications Earth & Environment, Nature Geoscience, The Holocene, GRL) and the Copernicus layer (Climate of the Past, Earth Surface Dynamics, Geochronology, Earth System Dynamics and EGUsphere preprints via the Copernicus member index). The three open-access Copernicus core journals of the profile held no new, not-already-listed papers in this window; their current issues had been covered the week before. One missing Crossref abstract (Communications Earth & Environment) was supplemented from the open OpenAlex index and treated as such.

What remains open. Elsevier deposits no abstracts with Crossref. The usual workaround via the publisher page was unavailable: ScienceDirect responds to this environment with a bot check, and the papers are days old and not yet indexed. Rather than inferring results from titles, those ten papers sit in a separate, clearly marked block without a score and without any claim about their content. For the scored entries the standard is unchanged: every substantive statement rests on an abstract that was inspected; preprints are flagged as such.
