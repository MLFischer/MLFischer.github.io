A weekly digest on Quaternary palaeoclimate research, East African rift dynamics, palaeohydrology and time-series analysis · 20 July 2026

What is GeoNews? An automatically generated weekly digest. Every Monday a research workflow screens the publications of the past one to two weeks along with funding, policy and community sources, scores the hits against a fixed set of criteria, and assembles this issue from the result.

The thematic profile is aligned with the research interests of Markus L. Fischer (Palaeoclimate Dynamics group, University of Potsdam): Quaternary palaeoclimate and palaeoenvironmental reconstruction, the East African Rift, the African Humid Period and human–environment coupling, lake-level and water-balance modelling, palaeofloods and fluvial geomorphology, ICDP drill cores, and linear and nonlinear time-series analysis. Anyone working in these fields will probably find something useful here — it is explicitly not a complete survey of Quaternary science.

Selection, scoring and summaries are produced by machine and reviewed before publication. Details may contain errors; where in doubt, the linked original source governs.

Each paper receives a score from 0 to 100 across five criteria: thematic fit (40), methodological fit (25), impact/venue (15), applicability (10) and recency (10). Categories: 75 and above, must-read · 50–74, relevant · 30–49, on the radar. The score measures proximity to this newsletter's profile and sets reading priority; it is not a quality judgement on the individual paper.

In brief

Five must-reads, and the week is unusually strong at the edges of the profile. From the core region comes a sediment-budget study of the Omo catchment and its contribution to the Turkana Depression across 27 million years — the same basin, a different timescale. Methodologically closest is a QSR paper on Mojave palaeohydrology during the last deglaciation, combining core sequences with shoreline archives; this is the reference architecture for robust lake-level reconstruction in arid basins. The most striking result: Nature Geoscience shows, from a 10,600-year Congo peat core, that dry phases cut the net oxygen budget by about 80 percent within centuries — abrupt, nonlinear, hydroclimatically triggered. Two palaeoflood papers follow, from the Yellow River and the Dadu Gorge.

On funding, the most important news of the year: the dedicated palaeoclimate call HORIZON-CL5-2027-01-D1-08 now has firm dates — opening 17 November 2026, deadline 4 March 2027, around €14 million for roughly two projects. At that success rate, consortium building is decided in the coming weeks, not in November. Alongside it: the ERC Starting Grant 2027 with a 15 October 2026 deadline and PAGES workshop applications due 11 September 2026.

Politically uncomfortable: the DFG has suspended approval of postdoc positions in Research Training Groups and pushed the next Priority Programme round to late 2026, with funding starting only in 2028. The draft revision of the German fixed-term academic contract law (WissZeitVG) introduces minimum contract durations of two years for postdocs but removes the carry-over provision — on balance a deterioration for the postdoc phase.

Part A — Papers of the week

Must-read (75 and above)

1. Paleohydrologic conditions in the Mojave Desert during the last deglaciation recorded by core-to-shore lacustrine records · Quaternary Science Reviews, 2026 · Score 93 Lake-level and water-balance reconstruction in an arid basin, built from the combination of drill core and shoreline or beach-ridge archives. This architecture — a core signal plus geomorphological evidence from the basin margin — is what allows lake levels to be anchored in absolute rather than merely relative terms, and it transfers directly to East African rift basins. The deglacial focus makes the paper additionally useful as a comparison case for termination dynamics. ⚠︎ Title-based (no abstract via Crossref/Elsevier). Theme 40 · Method 22 · Venue 11 · Applicability 10 · Recency 10 (created 18 Jul 2026) · DOI: 10.1016/j.quascirev.2026.110171

2. Hydroclimate controls Congo peatland net oxygen release over the past 10,600 years · Nature Geoscience, 2026 · Score 91 Direct redox titration applied to a 10,600-year peat core from the Congo Basin. The authors find systematic deviation from the conventionally assumed 1:1 oxygen-to-carbon stoichiometry and use it to quantify the net redox imbalance through time. The central finding is that the fluxes behave strongly nonlinearly: drier phases triggered abrupt declines in the oxygen surplus of about 80 percent within centuries, followed by rapid recovery once wetter conditions returned. Doubly relevant here — African Holocene hydroclimate on one hand, a well-documented case of threshold-like system response on the other. Theme 40 · Method 20 · Venue 15 · Applicability 10 · Recency 6 (created 10 Jul 2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s41561-026-02031-z

3. Palaeoflood Records from the Sanhuajian Reach in the Mid-lower Yellow River: Implications for Channel Evolution and Climatic Drivers · Palaeogeography, Palaeoclimatology, Palaeoecology, 2026 · Score 86 Palaeoflood reconstruction in a large river system, linked to channel evolution and climatic drivers. The methodological question is the same one that central European palaeoflood studies face, for instance on the Ahr: how to extrapolate robustly from slackwater and overbank deposits to discharge magnitudes, and how to separate the climatic signal from the geomorphological response of the system. ⚠︎ Title-based. Part B — Funding & calls

Horizon Europe — "Palaeoclimate science for a better understanding of Earth system dynamics" (HORIZON-CL5-2027-01-D1-08). Opening 17 November 2026, deadline 4 March 2027, Research and Innovation Action, budget around €14 million for roughly two projects, so about €7 million per consortium. The stated aim is robust information on palaeoclimate states and events outside the range of variability recorded over the past centuries, to guide the development of Earth system models. This is the tailor-made call previously only announced in the work programme; with two funded projects Europe-wide the competition is severe, and consortium discussions belong early in the timeline. Sources: topic page · CL5 Work Programme 2026–2027 (PDF).

ERC Starting Grant 2027 — deadline 15 October 2026. Step 1 results in April 2027, interviews and Step 2 results in August 2027. Eligibility: PhD defended no more than ten years before 1 January 2027, extendable for parental and care leave. New from the 2027 calls: at most one Starting and one Consolidator Grant per career, plus stricter application rules. The ERC Consolidator 2027 is expected in January 2027. Sources: ERC — Applying in the 2027 competitions · ERC — Changes to the 2026 and 2027 Work Programmes · Science|Business.

Part C — Research policy (Germany/EU)

DFG cuts at the postdoc level. Facing high collective-agreement pay rises and increased equipment and consumables costs that the guaranteed annual funding increments do not cover, the DFG has postponed the funding start for new Research Training Group applications and suspended approval of postdoc positions. For postdoc career paths in the geosciences this removes one funding route for the time being, increasing pressure on individual grants and EU money. Source: Fakultätentag Informatik.

Federal budget 2027 — cabinet decision expected 29 July 2026. The research ministry's budget remains nearly stable against the April planning, around €70 million below it, making it an exception compared with other departmental budgets; growth in the federal budget goes largely to defence. As interpretation rather than fact: stability at a high nominal level amounts, given current pay and materials cost increases, to real stagnation — consistent with what the DFG reports operationally. Source: Wiarda blog, 7 July 2026.

WissZeitVG — ministry draft published. The core of the draft is minimum contract durations of three years for first contracts of doctoral researchers and two years for postdocs in the respective qualification phase. The maximum fixed-term duration remains six years per phase; the "four plus two" arrangement from the failed previous draft is off the table. Unfavourable for the postdoc phase: the previous carry-over provision is removed, under which unused fixed-term time from the doctoral phase could be transferred. Assessments diverge widely, from "timid minimal compromise" to criticism from the university side. Practical consequence: anyone carrying unused time from the doctoral phase should check how the draft affects their personal fixed-term account. Sources: Wiarda blog · Forschung & Lehre · fzs statement · DPG statement.

DFG annual meeting, 1 July 2026. A clear statement in defence of the freedom and independence of science, combined with criticism of the AfD's science policy. In parallel, five new Research Units were established with around €27 million in total, and five were extended. Source: DFG.

Method & sources

Paper screening uses the Crossref REST API with topic- and ISSN-based queries and the from-created-date filter. Abstracts, where available, come from Crossref; Elsevier generally does not supply them, and those entries are marked ⚠︎ title-based. DOIs already listed in an earlier issue are not repeated. Funding, research policy and deadlines rest on the sources linked in Parts B to D.

Limitation of this issue: direct API access from the compute environment was blocked; the Crossref queries ran through the browser instead — the same source, but with a reduced number of results per query. Coverage is therefore somewhat lower than usual and very recent registrations may be missing. ScienceDirect additionally blocked retrieval of publisher abstracts, which raises the share of title-based assessments above the normal level.
