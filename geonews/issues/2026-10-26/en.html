What is GeoNews? An automatically compiled weekly digest. A research workflow scans publications from the past one to two weeks as well as funding, policy and community sources, scores the hits against a fixed set of criteria and assembles this issue from them.

The thematic profile follows the research interests of Markus L. Fischer (Palaeoclimate Dynamics group, University of Potsdam): Quaternary palaeoclimate and palaeoenvironmental reconstruction, the East African Rift, the African Humid Period and human–environment coupling, lake-level and water-balance modelling, palaeofloods and fluvial geomorphology, ICDP drill cores, and linear and nonlinear time-series analysis. Researchers in these fields will probably find something useful here — it is explicitly not a complete overview of Quaternary science.

Selection, scoring and summaries are machine-generated and reviewed before publication. Details may contain errors; when in doubt, the linked original source prevails.

Each paper receives a score of 0–100 from five criteria: topical fit (40), methodological fit (25), impact/venue (15), connectivity (10) and recency (10). Categories: 75 and above must-read · 50–74 relevant · 30–49 radar. The score measures proximity to this newsletter's profile and prioritises reading — it is not a quality judgement on the individual paper.

In brief

Around forty papers assessed, this week six must-reads, all abstract-based. A primary hit directly from the East African Rift is again missing; instead the focus is on monsoon and human–environment coupling on orbital timescales and on lake water balance and palaeoflood calibration.

At the top: a 500-kyr dust record from the western equatorial Indian Ocean (Nature Communications) which, according to the abstract, links hominin population expansions in Arabia not only to "Green Arabia" humid phases but also to brief high-intensity rainfall phases within glacials; and a 450-kyr speleothem record of the South American Summer Monsoon (Nature) that mostly runs out of phase with the Asian monsoon but exceeds a synchronicity threshold in ten events. The other : a dual-isotope water balance (P−E) for Lake Junín since the LGM, 439 kyr of crater-lake sediments from Rodderberg (East Eifel), a process check of flood turbidites against monitoring data in Lake Lucerne, and ten lake cores from the Kasaï Basin (Congo) suggesting that the Late Holocene rainforest crisis was more widespread than assumed.

In the block: White Sands (humans and the abrupt desiccation of a pluvial lake during the LGM), a re-dated Early Weichselian sequence in Lower Saxony (Oerel/Glinde → MIS 5a/4), a perspective paper on process-informed early warning systems and a PNAS approach to tipping-point forecasting with neural operators.

Funding: ICDP AGU travel grants (8 Nov), opening of the Horizon Europe palaeoclimate call (17 Nov), ERC Consolidator (12 Jan 2027), ICDP proposals (15 Jan 2027). Policy: Germany's Bundestag research committee was scheduled on 14 Oct to decide on a public hearing on the WissZeitVG reform (no date published at the time of writing); the budget committee's final session on the 2027 federal budget is on 12 Nov, the final vote on 27 Nov; the European Parliament adopted its position on the 2027 EU budget on 21 Oct (reportedly +€63 million for Horizon Europe).

Must-read (75 and above)

1. Arabian hydroclimate and hominin population dynamics over the last 500,000 years · Nature Communications, 2026 · Score 83 A multi-proxy, high-resolution dust record from the western equatorial Indian Ocean spanning the last 500 kyr, combined with hunter-gatherer demographic data. Findings per abstract: the record supports the view that hominin presence in Arabia was tied to enhanced monsoon precipitation during "Green Arabia" periods, with marked population expansions during prolonged wetter periods. Occupation was, however, not linked exclusively to classic humid periods: brief phases of high-intensity rainfall within some glacials — expressed as transient high dust contents due to more terrestrial moisture and more wind-erodible material — also favoured expansions according to the authors. Interpretation: high dust is not read uniformly as "dry" here — a methodologically relevant point for interpreting dust and terrigenous proxies in northeast African–Arabian archives. Directly connected to the question of how humid phases and dispersal corridors between East Africa and Arabia relate. Topic 38 · Method 16 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 25 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-78983-y

2. Interhemispheric monsoon dynamics over the past 450,000 years · Nature, 2026 · Score 83 A speleothem δ¹⁸O record from the Amazon Basin documents South American Summer Monsoon (SASM) variability over 450 kyr. Findings per abstract: monsoons in both hemispheres vary with Milankovitch forcing and internal boundary conditions; SASM and the Asian monsoon have been strikingly out of phase, but occasionally behave synchronously. The authors define a synchronicity threshold that is exceeded during ten events, including glacial terminations; comparison with forcing factors points to greenhouse gases, cross-equatorial thermal gradients and Atlantic/Pacific SSTs as probable influences (interpretation, not a causal test). Methodologically close to questions about phase relationships and regime shifts between proxy series; useful for placing African monsoon records in the global monsoon system. Topic 36 · Method 20 · Venue 15 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 6 (created 15 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1038/s41586-026-11166-3

3. Dual biomarker δ²H and δ¹³C records allow deconvolution of precipitation and evaporation signals in Lake Junin sediments since the last glacial maximum · Quaternary Science Reviews, 2026 · Score 81 At Lake Junín (central Andes) the authors separate the precipitation signal (terrestrial nC₂₉ alkanes) from the evaporation signal (aquatic nC₂₃ alkanes) using two independent isotope systems (δ²H_wax, δ¹³C_wax); brGDGTs constrain the temperature–drought relationship. Findings per abstract: the water-balance reconstruction shows high sensitivity of the central Andes to monsoon intensity, driven by both large-scale circulation (P) and local conditions (E); significant variations in evaporative enrichment over ~22 kyr, with reduced P−E balance and local water stress particularly in the middle Holocene. Directly relevant for lake water-balance models: a proxy approach that addresses P and E separately provides exactly the constraints otherwise missing in water-balance calculations for closed lake basins. Topic 36 · Method 18 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 19 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1016/j.quascirev.2026.110341

4. Climate-modulated accumulation and erosion of sediments in the Rodderberg crater basin for the last 439 ka (East Eifel Volcanic Field, Germany) · Journal of Quaternary Science, 2026 · Score 80 A discontinuous 73-m sediment record from the Rodderberg crater with high-resolution grain-size and geochemical data, dated by radiocarbon, luminescence and tephrochronology, reaching back into the Middle Pleistocene (439 ka). Findings per abstract: laminated, sand- and organic-rich sediments during interglacials, layered silt-rich loess derivatives during glacials; the crater desiccated during MIS 8 and MIS 5, eroding the MIS 9 and MIS 5e interglacials. Sequence according to the authors: deep crater lake (MIS 12–10), desiccation (MIS 8), shallow lake (MIS 7), desiccating wetland (MIS 6), followed by loess and a Holocene soil. An important long record for central European lake archives — and a case study in how erosional gaps can bias the interpretation of long lake cores. Topic 36 · Method 18 · Venue 8 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 22 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1002/jqs.70131

5. Intense rainstorms as drivers of lacustrine turbidity currents: implications for paleoflood records · Sedimentologika, 2026 · Score 79 Process study at the subaqueous Muota delta (Lake Lucerne): hydro-meteorological catchment data, moored ADCP measurements of turbidity currents and sediment cores covering ~50 years. Findings per abstract: the largest floods correspond clearly to thick, extensive turbidites, whereas moderate floods leave variable or no deposits. Turbidity-current initiation is governed not by discharge alone but by suspended sediment concentration — and thus by rainfall characteristics and sediment mobilisation. Short high-intensity rainstorms can trigger discharge-decoupled turbidity currents, while some higher but less turbid discharge peaks do not. The authors recommend process-based calibration with monitoring data. Directly relevant for interpreting lacustrine palaeoflood archives: deposit thickness is not a linear discharge proxy. Topic 40 · Method 16 · Venue 5 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 21 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.57035/journals/sdk.2026.e42.2473

6. Erosional Transfer of Sediment and Organic Carbon Through Time in the Kasaï Basin, Democratic Republic of the Congo · Paleoceanography and Paleoclimatology, 2026 · Score 78 Sediment cores from ten lakes along the rainforest–savanna boundary in the Kasaï Basin; apparent sediment accumulation rates, TOC and bulk density yield organic-carbon mass accumulation rates. Findings per abstract: most lakes show reduced accumulation during the Late Holocene Rainforest Crisis (~3000 yr BP), mirroring peat cores from the Cuvette Centrale; according to the authors the crisis was thus more widespread than previously postulated, not limited to central-western Africa. Carbon deposited over the last ~5000 cal yr BP in two representative records is highly susceptible to remineralisation upon oxygen exposure. Connects to the question of how spatially coherent the end of the African Humid Period was in equatorial Africa. Topic 34 · Method 15 · Venue 11 · Connectivity 8 · Recency 10 (created 22 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1029/2026pa005609

Relevant (50–74)

7. Humans experienced abrupt environmental changes at White Sands during the Last Glacial Maximum · Science Advances, 2026 · Score 74 Expanded excavations at White Sands National Park: 20 horizons with in-situ human and megafaunal footprints. Radiocarbon dating of conifer pollen plus aquatic seeds refines the chronology to 23.5–21.7 ka. Findings per abstract: biomarkers, pollen and stratigraphy show a turnover from a perennial lake to a playa and a major aridification episode coincident with Dansgaard–Oeschger event 2 (23.2 ka), which shrank pluvial Lake Otero and allowed people to traverse the former shoreline for nearly two millennia. A human–environment case at a shrinking lake — structurally comparable to questions asked of East African rift lakes. Topic 32 · Method 15 · Venue 15 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 6 (created 14 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aeh0707

8. Environmental dynamics and timing of the late Eemian interglacial to the Weichselian pleniglacial at Lichtenberg, Germany: implications for the Oerel and Glinde interstadials · Boreas, 2026 · Score 72 Multi-proxy record (sedimentology, geochemistry, palynology) from two cores at Lichtenberg (Lower Saxony), next to a Middle Palaeolithic site, with new OSL ages. Findings per abstract: two previously unresolved oscillations (Dangenstorf I/II) with dwarf-birch shrub tundra, correlated with the Oerel and Glinde interstadials; per OSL placed at the MIS 5a/4 transition and assigned to Greenland Interstadials GI 20 and 19.2 — not MIS 3 as previously assumed. Relevant for chronostratigraphy and correlation of central European archives with North Atlantic variability. Topic 32 · Method 16 · Venue 8 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 19 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1111/bor.70078

9. Ideas for Creating Early Warning Systems in the Climate Sciences · EGUsphere (preprint, in discussion), 2026 · Score 72 Perspective and review: generic early-warning indicators (e.g. critical slowing down) make little use of process understanding; the authors argue for process-informed early warning systems whose structure reflects the dynamics of the system in question — drawing on Earth observation (space-for-time approaches), meteorological forcing data, reduced dynamical models and ESM process descriptions. No new data; conceptually relevant for tipping-point analyses of palaeorecords, where the same question of generic vs. system-specific indicators arises. Not yet peer-reviewed. Topic 30 · Method 22 · Venue 4 · Connectivity 6 · Recency 10 (created 20 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.5194/egusphere-2026-5606

10. Tipping point forecasting in nonstationary dynamics on function spaces · PNAS, 2026 · Score 72 A recurrent neural operator (RNO) is trained only on pre-tipping dynamics; a conformal-prediction framework forecasts tipping points from deviations from physics constraints (conserved quantities, PDEs) with an uncertainty measure. Tested on Lorenz-63, Kuramoto–Sivashinsky, a stratocumulus tipping point and flow-stall transitions. Findings per abstract: even partial or approximate physics constraints suffice for accurate forecasts. Methodologically interesting, but only of limited transferability to proxy series without known equations of motion (assessment). Topic 30 · Method 18 · Venue 15 · Connectivity 3 · Recency 6 (created 15 Oct 2026) · DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2600331123

Methods & sources

Paper screening follows the fixed set of criteria (topical fit, methodological fit, impact/venue, connectivity, recency) and excludes DOIs already listed in a previous issue. Funding opportunities, science policy and dates are based on the sources linked in Parts B to D.

What this means for coverage. Where neither OpenAlex nor Crossref holds an abstract, the entry is marked ⚠︎ title-based; no results beyond the title were invented. Dates given are Crossref created dates (registration), which may differ from the online publication date by a few days. Topic queries return a lot of off-topic noise; pre-selection was by keywords and then manual, so individual relevant papers outside the core journals may be missing.
